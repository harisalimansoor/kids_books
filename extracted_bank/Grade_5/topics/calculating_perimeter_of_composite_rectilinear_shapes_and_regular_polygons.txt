=== Topic: Calculating perimeter of composite rectilinear shapes and regular polygons ===
Domain: Measurement

--- Calculating perimeter of composite rectilinear shapes ---

Question 1: An L-shaped room has six straight walls meeting at right angles. The lengths of the walls are 7 m, 9 m, 3 m, 4 m, 4 m, and 5 m. Calculate the perimeter of the room in metres.
Answer: 32 m
Working: Add all the given side lengths together: 7 + 9 + 3 + 4 + 4 + 5 = 32 m.

Question 2: An L-shaped lawn has edges meeting at right angles. The longest horizontal side along the bottom is 14 m and the longest vertical side along the left is 11 m. The top horizontal side is 6 m and the right vertical side is 4 m. What is the total perimeter of the lawn in metres?
Answer: 50 m
Working: The missing inner vertical side is 11 m - 4 m = 7 m. The missing inner horizontal side is 14 m - 6 m = 8 m. Total perimeter = 14 + 11 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 4 = 50 m.

Question 3: Four identical square tiles, each with sides of 8 cm, are placed in a straight row to make a single rectangle. What is the perimeter of the resulting rectangle in centimetres?
Answer: 80 cm
Working: The length of the new rectangle is 4 × 8 cm = 32 cm. The width is 8 cm. Perimeter = 2 × (32 + 8) = 2 × 40 = 80 cm.

Question 4: A rectilinear patio has a horizontal base of 4 m and a total vertical height on the left of 350 cm. The top horizontal edge is 180 cm, and the right vertical edge is 150 cm. The shape is an L-shape with all right-angled turns. What is the perimeter of the patio in centimetres?
Answer: 1500 cm
Working: Convert the base to centimetres: 4 m = 400 cm. The missing inner vertical edge is 350 cm - 150 cm = 200 cm. The missing inner horizontal edge is 400 cm - 180 cm = 220 cm. Total perimeter = 400 + 350 + 180 + 200 + 220 + 150 = 1500 cm.

Question 5: A T-shaped flowerbed has right-angled edges. The top horizontal bar is 6 m wide and 2 m deep. A rectangular stem of width 2 m is attached centrally beneath the bar and extends downwards by 5 m. What is the perimeter of the flowerbed in metres?
Answer: 26 m
Working: Top edge = 6 m. Two outer vertical edges of top bar = 2 m each. Two undersides of top bar = (6 - 2) ÷ 2 = 2 m each. Two vertical sides of stem = 5 m each. Bottom edge of stem = 2 m. Total perimeter = 6 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 5 + 5 + 2 = 26 m.

Question 6: A plus-sign shape is made by joining five identical squares of side length 6 cm: one central square and four outer squares attached to each of its four sides. What is the total perimeter of this composite shape in centimetres?
Answer: 72 cm
Working: Each of the 4 outer squares has 3 exposed edges. Total number of outer edges = 4 × 3 = 12. Perimeter = 12 × 6 cm = 72 cm.

--- Calculating perimeter of regular polygons ---

Question 7: A regular pentagon has sides of length 9 cm. What is the perimeter of the pentagon in centimetres?
Answer: 45 cm
Working: A regular pentagon has 5 equal sides. Perimeter = 5 × 9 cm = 45 cm.

Question 8: A regular hexagon has sides of length 14 m. Calculate its perimeter in metres.
Answer: 84 m
Working: A regular hexagon has 6 equal sides. Perimeter = 6 × 14 m = 84 m.

Question 9: A regular octagon has a perimeter of 72 cm. What is the length of one of its sides in centimetres?
Answer: 9 cm
Working: A regular octagon has 8 equal sides. Side length = 72 cm ÷ 8 = 9 cm.

Question 10: A regular decagon has a side length of 3.8 cm. Calculate the perimeter of the decagon in centimetres.
Answer: 38 cm
Working: A regular decagon has 10 equal sides. Perimeter = 10 × 3.8 cm = 38 cm.

Question 11: An equilateral triangle has a perimeter of 36 cm. A regular nonagon has sides of the same length as the equilateral triangle. What is the perimeter of the regular nonagon in centimetres?
Answer: 108 cm
Working: Side length of the equilateral triangle = 36 cm ÷ 3 = 12 cm. A regular nonagon has 9 equal sides of 12 cm. Perimeter = 9 × 12 cm = 108 cm.

Question 12: A regular heptagon has sides measuring 6.5 cm each. What is the perimeter of the heptagon in centimetres?
Answer: 45.5 cm
Working: A regular heptagon has 7 equal sides. Perimeter = 7 × 6.5 cm = 45.5 cm.

Question 13: A regular pentagon and a regular hexagon both have sides of length 7 cm. They are placed next to each other so that they share one complete edge without overlapping. What is the perimeter of the outer boundary of this combined shape in centimetres?
Answer: 63 cm
Working: The regular pentagon has 5 - 1 = 4 outer edges. The regular hexagon has 6 - 1 = 5 outer edges. Total outer edges = 4 + 5 = 9 edges. Perimeter = 9 × 7 cm = 63 cm.
