=== Topic: Prime numbers up to 100, prime factors, and composite number classification ===
Domain: Multiplication and Division

--- Prime numbers up to 100 ---

Question 1: What is the only even prime number?
Answer: 2
Working: A prime number has exactly two distinct factors: 1 and itself. 2 has factors 1 and 2, making it prime. Every other even number is divisible by 2 and therefore has more than two factors.

Question 2: List all the prime numbers between 10 and 20.
Answer: 11, 13, 17, 19
Working: Check each whole number between 10 and 20: 11 has only factors 1 and 11; 12 is divisible by 2; 13 has only factors 1 and 13; 14 is divisible by 2; 15 is divisible by 3 and 5; 16 is divisible by 2; 17 has only factors 1 and 17; 18 is divisible by 2; 19 has only factors 1 and 19. The prime numbers are 11, 13, 17, and 19.

Question 3: What is the smallest prime number greater than 30?
Answer: 31
Working: Test whole numbers starting from 31: 31 is not divisible by 2, 3, or 5. Its only factors are 1 and 31, so 31 is the smallest prime number greater than 30.

Question 4: Which two numbers from this list are prime numbers: 23, 27, 33, 37, 39?
Answer: 23 and 37
Working: Check each number: 23 has factors 1 and 23 (prime); 27 = 3 × 9 (not prime); 33 = 3 × 11 (not prime); 37 has factors 1 and 37 (prime); 39 = 3 × 13 (not prime). The two prime numbers are 23 and 37.

Question 5: Calculate the sum of the first four prime numbers.
Answer: 17
Working: The first four prime numbers are 2, 3, 5, and 7. Calculate their sum: 2 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 17.

Question 6: A prime number is between 40 and 50. The sum of its digits is 11. What is the number?
Answer: 47
Working: The prime numbers between 40 and 50 are 41, 43, and 47. Check digit sums: 4 + 1 = 5; 4 + 3 = 7; 4 + 7 = 11. The required prime number is 47.

Question 7: Is 97 a prime number? Write yes or no.
Answer: yes
Working: Test prime divisors up to the square root of 97 (less than 10): 97 is not divisible by 2 (odd), not divisible by 3 (9 + 7 = 16), not divisible by 5 (does not end in 0 or 5), and not divisible by 7 (97 ÷ 7 = 13 r 6). Thus, 97 has only two factors: 1 and 97, so it is prime.

--- Prime factors ---

Question 8: The factors of 28 are 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, and 28. Which of these factors are prime numbers?
Answer: 2 and 7
Working: Check the prime status of each factor: 1 is neither prime nor composite; 2 is prime; 4 is composite; 7 is prime; 14 is composite; 28 is composite. The prime factors are 2 and 7.

Question 9: List all the prime factors of 18.
Answer: 2 and 3
Working: The factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 18. Among these, the prime numbers are 2 and 3.

Question 10: Write 20 as a product of its prime factors.
Answer: 2 × 2 × 5
Working: Decompose 20 into prime factors: 20 = 2 × 10 = 2 × 2 × 5.

Question 11: A mystery number is the product of three prime factors: 2, 3, and 7. What is the mystery number?
Answer: 42
Working: Multiply the prime factors together: 2 × 3 = 6, and 6 × 7 = 42.

Question 12: What is the only prime number that is a factor of both 15 and 35?
Answer: 5
Working: Prime factors of 15 are 3 and 5. Prime factors of 35 are 5 and 7. The common prime factor is 5.

Question 13: Write 60 as a product of prime factors.
Answer: 2 × 2 × 3 × 5
Working: Break 60 down into factors: 60 = 6 × 10 = (2 × 3) × (2 × 5) = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.

--- Composite number classification ---

Question 14: What is the smallest composite number?
Answer: 4
Working: Check whole numbers starting from 1: 1 has 1 factor (neither prime nor composite); 2 has 2 factors (prime); 3 has 2 factors (prime); 4 has 3 factors (1, 2, 4), so 4 is the smallest composite number.

Question 15: Is 39 a prime number or a composite number?
Answer: composite number
Working: The factors of 39 are 1, 3, 13, and 39 (since 3 × 13 = 39). Because it has more than two factors, 39 is a composite number.

Question 16: List all the composite numbers between 10 and 16.
Answer: 12, 14, 15
Working: The numbers between 10 and 16 are 11, 12, 13, 14, 15. 11 and 13 are prime. 12 (factors 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12), 14 (factors 1, 2, 7, 14), and 15 (factors 1, 3, 5, 15) all have more than two factors, so they are composite.

Question 17: Which number in this list is NOT a composite number: 9, 15, 21, 25, 29, 33?
Answer: 29
Working: Check the factors of each number: 9 = 3 × 3 (composite); 15 = 3 × 5 (composite); 21 = 3 × 7 (composite); 25 = 5 × 5 (composite); 29 has only 1 and 29 as factors (prime); 33 = 3 × 11 (composite). The number that is not composite is 29.

Question 18: Maya says, "All odd numbers greater than 1 are prime numbers." Give one odd number between 1 and 20 that proves Maya is incorrect.
Answer: 9
Working: To disprove the statement, find an odd composite number between 1 and 20. 9 is odd and composite (3 × 3 = 9). (15 is also an odd composite number: 3 × 5 = 15).

Question 19: What is the smallest 2-digit composite number that has 5 as a factor?
Answer: 10
Working: The smallest 2-digit number is 10. Check if 10 is composite and has 5 as a factor: factors of 10 are 1, 2, 5, and 10. Since it has 4 factors, it is composite and 5 is a factor. Thus, 10 is the answer.
