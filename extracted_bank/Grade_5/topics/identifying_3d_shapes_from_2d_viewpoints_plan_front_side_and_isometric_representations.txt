=== Topic: Identifying 3D shapes from 2D viewpoints (plan, front, side) and isometric representations ===
Domain: Geometry: Properties of Shapes

--- Identifying 3D shapes from 2D viewpoints (plan, front, side) ---

Question 1: A 3D shape has a circular plan view (view from directly above), a triangular front view, and a triangular side view. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Cone
Working: A shape with a circular base (plan view) that tapers to a point, showing triangles from the front and side, is a cone.

Question 2: A 3D shape looks like a circle from the plan view, from the front view, and from the side view. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Sphere
Working: A sphere is completely round and looks like a circle from every 2D viewpoint.

Question 3: A 3D shape has a square plan view, a square front view, and a square side view. All faces are identical squares. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Cube
Working: A shape whose plan, front, and side views are all identical squares is a cube.

Question 4: A 3D shape has a circular plan view, a rectangular front view, and a rectangular side view. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Cylinder
Working: A cylinder has two circular parallel ends (circular plan view) and straight parallel sides (rectangular front and side views).

Question 5: A 3D shape has a square plan view, a triangular front view, and a triangular side view. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Square-based pyramid
Working: The plan view shows a square base, and the front and side views show triangular faces meeting at a vertex, which describes a square-based pyramid.

Question 6: A prism resting on one of its rectangular faces has a triangular front view, a rectangular plan view, and a rectangular side view. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Triangular prism
Working: A shape with uniform triangular cross-sections and rectangular sides is a triangular prism.

Question 7: A solid wooden block has a plan view measuring 5 cm by 3 cm, a front view measuring 5 cm by 2 cm, and a side view measuring 3 cm by 2 cm. All six faces are rectangles. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Cuboid
Working: A 3D shape with three pairs of rectangular faces with dimensions 5 cm by 3 cm, 5 cm by 2 cm, and 3 cm by 2 cm is a cuboid.

Question 8: A 3D shape stands on a horizontal table. Its plan view is a regular hexagon. Its front and side views are both triangles that meet at a single top vertex. Name this 3D shape.
Answer: Hexagonal-based pyramid
Working: The base is a regular hexagon (plan view) and the triangular faces meet at an apex, making it a hexagonal-based pyramid (or hexagonal pyramid).

--- Identifying 3D shapes from isometric representations ---

Question 9: A model drawn on isometric dot paper is built using 1 cm³ cubes. It has a bottom row of 4 cubes and 2 cubes placed directly on top of the first two cubes. How many 1 cm³ cubes were used to make this model?
Answer: 6
Working: Bottom row = 4 cubes. Top row = 2 cubes. Total cubes = 4 + 2 = 6 cubes.

Question 10: A solid cuboid drawn on isometric paper is 4 cubes long, 3 cubes wide, and 2 cubes high. How many 1 cm³ cubes are needed in total to build this cuboid?
Answer: 24
Working: Number of cubes in the base layer = 4 × 3 = 12 cubes. For 2 layers: 12 × 2 = 24 cubes.

Question 11: A tiered tower drawn on an isometric grid is made of 1 cm³ cubes. The bottom layer is a 3 by 3 square of cubes. The second layer is a 2 by 2 square of cubes placed centrally on top. The top layer is a single cube. How many centimetre cubes are used in total?
Answer: 14
Working: Bottom layer = 3 × 3 = 9 cubes. Second layer = 2 × 2 = 4 cubes. Top layer = 1 cube. Total = 9 + 4 + 1 = 14 cubes.

Question 12: A staircase model drawn on isometric dot paper has 3 steps. The first step is 1 cube high, the second step is 2 cubes high, and the third step is 3 cubes high. Each step is 2 cubes wide. How many centimetre cubes were used in total to make this model?
Answer: 12
Working: Number of cubes in one cross-section of the stairs = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 cubes. Since the staircase is 2 cubes wide: 6 × 2 = 12 cubes.

Question 13: A symmetrical structure drawn on an isometric grid is built from 1 cm³ cubes. It has a base layer of 5 cubes in a straight line, a middle layer of 3 cubes centred on top of the base, and a top layer of 1 cube centred on top of the middle layer. What is the total volume of the structure in cm³?
Answer: 9 cm³
Working: Base layer = 5 cm³. Middle layer = 3 cm³. Top layer = 1 cm³. Total volume = 5 + 3 + 1 = 9 cm³.

Question 14: A solid cube measuring 3 cm by 3 cm by 3 cm is built from twenty-seven 1 cm³ cubes. A builder removes the centre cube from each of the 6 outer faces, and also removes the single cube in the very centre of the large cube. How many 1 cm³ cubes remain?
Answer: 20
Working: Total starting cubes = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cubes. Number of cubes removed = 6 face centres + 1 central cube = 7 cubes. Remaining cubes = 27 - 7 = 20 cubes.
