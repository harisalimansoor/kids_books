=== Topic: Reading, writing, ordering and comparing numbers up to 1 000 000 ===
Domain: Number and Place Value

--- Reading numbers up to 1 000 000 ---

Question 1: Write the number 47 308 in words.
Answer: forty-seven thousand, three hundred and eight
Working: The thousands part is 47 (forty-seven thousand). The hundreds, tens, and ones are 308 (three hundred and eight). Combined, this is forty-seven thousand, three hundred and eight.

Question 2: Write the number 605 012 in words.
Answer: six hundred and five thousand and twelve
Working: The thousands group is 605 (six hundred and five thousand). There are 0 hundreds and 12 in the tens and ones (twelve). Combined, this is six hundred and five thousand and twelve.

Question 3: In the number 842 519, what is the value of the digit 4?
Answer: 40 000
Working: The digit 4 is in the ten thousands column, so its value is 4 × 10 000 = 40 000 (forty thousand).

Question 4: In the number 930 241, what is the value of the digit 9?
Answer: 900 000
Working: The digit 9 is in the hundred thousands column, so its value is 9 × 100 000 = 900 000 (nine hundred thousand).

--- Writing numbers up to 1 000 000 ---

Question 5: Write the number 'sixty-two thousand, four hundred and fifteen' in digits.
Answer: 62 415
Working: Sixty-two thousand is 62 000. Four hundred and fifteen is 415. Together, the number is 62 415.

Question 6: Write the number 'eight hundred thousand, six hundred and four' in digits.
Answer: 800 604
Working: Eight hundred thousand gives 800 in the thousands period. Six hundred and four gives 604 in the hundreds, tens and ones period. The number is 800 604.

Question 7: Write the number 'four hundred and seventeen thousand and fifty' in digits.
Answer: 417 050
Working: Four hundred and seventeen thousand is 417 000. Fifty is 050. Written together, it is 417 050.

Question 8: Write one million in digits.
Answer: 1 000 000
Working: One million is written as a 1 followed by six zeros: 1 000 000.

Question 9: A number has 7 hundred thousands, 0 ten thousands, 4 thousands, 9 hundreds, 0 tens, and 3 ones. Write this number in digits.
Answer: 704 903
Working: Placing the digits in order of place value (HTh, TTh, Th, H, T, O): 7, 0, 4, 9, 0, 3 gives 704 903.

Question 10: Using each of the digits 3, 5, 8, 0, 1 and 9 exactly once, write the largest possible 6-digit odd number.
Answer: 985 301
Working: To make the number odd, the last digit must be an odd digit (1, 3, 5, or 9). To make the number as large as possible, use the largest available digits in the highest place value columns: 9 in HTh, 8 in TTh, 5 in Th, 3 in H, 0 in T, and 1 in O. 985 301 is odd and the largest possible.

--- Ordering numbers up to 1 000 000 ---

Question 11: Order these numbers starting with the smallest: 45 200, 42 500, 54 200, 42 050.
Answer: 42 050, 42 500, 45 200, 54 200
Working: Compare ten thousands and thousands: 42 050 and 42 500 are the smallest; 42 050 < 42 500. Next is 45 200, and the largest is 54 200. The order from smallest to largest is 42 050, 42 500, 45 200, 54 200.

Question 12: Order these numbers in descending order (largest first): 350 200, 305 200, 352 000, 305 020.
Answer: 352 000, 350 200, 305 200, 305 020
Working: Compare the numbers: 352 000 is the largest, followed by 350 200. Next, compare 305 200 and 305 020: 305 200 > 305 020. So the descending order is 352 000, 350 200, 305 200, 305 020.

Question 13: A museum recorded its annual visitor numbers over four years: 618 450, 681 405, 618 540, 681 045. Write these visitor numbers in ascending order (smallest first).
Answer: 618 450, 618 540, 681 045, 681 405
Working: Compare hundred thousands and ten thousands: 618 450 and 618 540 are smaller than 681 045 and 681 405. Comparing 618 450 and 618 540: 450 < 540, so 618 450 < 618 540. Comparing 681 045 and 681 405: 045 < 405, so 681 045 < 681 405. Ascending order: 618 450, 618 540, 681 045, 681 405.

Question 14: Four runners logged their total steps in a year: Runner A logged 745 820 steps, Runner B logged 754 280 steps, Runner C logged 745 280 steps, and Runner D logged 754 802 steps. List the runners in order from most steps to fewest steps.
Answer: Runner D, Runner B, Runner A, Runner C
Working: Compare step counts from largest to smallest: 754 802 (Runner D) > 754 280 (Runner B) > 745 820 (Runner A) > 745 280 (Runner C).

--- Comparing numbers up to 1 000 000 ---

Question 15: Write <, > or = to make the statement true: 345 670 [ ] 354 670
Answer: <
Working: Both numbers have 3 hundred thousands. Compare the ten thousands digits: 4 ten thousands (40 000) is less than 5 ten thousands (50 000). Therefore, 345 670 < 354 670.

Question 16: Write <, > or = to make the statement true: 700 000 + 40 000 + 800 [ ] 740 080
Answer: >
Working: Calculate the left side: 700 000 + 40 000 + 800 = 740 800. Compare 740 800 and 740 080: 800 is greater than 80, so 740 800 > 740 080.

Question 17: Stadium A holds 125 400 people. Stadium B holds 125 040 people. Which stadium has the greater capacity?
Answer: Stadium A
Working: Both capacities have 125 thousands. Compare the hundreds: 125 400 has 4 hundreds, while 125 040 has 0 hundreds. 125 400 > 125 040, so Stadium A has the greater capacity.

Question 18: Look at the inequality: 456 320 < 45_ 320 < 459 320. Which single digits could replace the blank space to make the statement correct?
Answer: 7 or 8
Working: All three numbers share the same hundred thousands (4), ten thousands (5), hundreds (3), tens (2), and ones (0). We compare the thousands digits: 6 < _ < 9. The digits strictly between 6 and 9 are 7 and 8.

Question 19: Which number is greater: 5 hundred thousands and 4 tens, or 504 000? Write the greater number in digits.
Answer: 504 000
Working: 5 hundred thousands and 4 tens = 500 040. Comparing 500 040 and 504 000: 504 000 has 4 thousands, whereas 500 040 has 0 thousands. Therefore, 504 000 is greater.
