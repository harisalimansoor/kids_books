=== Topic: Rounding decimals to the nearest whole number and to 1 decimal place ===
Domain: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

--- Rounding decimals to the nearest whole number ---

Question 1: Round 6.4 to the nearest whole number.
Answer: 6
Working: The tenths digit is 4, which is less than 5, so round down to 6.

Question 2: Round 8.7 to the nearest whole number.
Answer: 9
Working: The tenths digit is 7, which is 5 or more, so round up to 9.

Question 3: Round 14.52 to the nearest whole number.
Answer: 15
Working: Look at the tenths digit, which is 5. Since it is 5 or more, round up to 15.

Question 4: Round 0.83 to the nearest whole number.
Answer: 1
Working: The tenths digit is 8, which is 5 or more, so round up from 0 to 1.

Question 5: A runner completes a sprint in 23.48 seconds. Round this time to the nearest whole second.
Answer: 23 seconds
Working: The tenths digit is 4, which is less than 5, so round down to 23 seconds.

Question 6: A bag of flour has a mass of 3.49 kg. Round the mass of the bag of flour to the nearest whole kilogram.
Answer: 3 kg
Working: To round to the nearest whole number, look at the tenths digit (4). Since 4 is less than 5, round down to 3 kg.

Question 7: Here are four decimal numbers: 7.09, 7.51, 6.49, 6.52. Write down the two numbers that round to 7 when rounded to the nearest whole number.
Answer: 7.09 and 6.52
Working: 7.09 rounds down to 7 (tenths digit is 0). 7.51 rounds up to 8 (tenths digit is 5). 6.49 rounds down to 6 (tenths digit is 4). 6.52 rounds up to 7 (tenths digit is 5). The two numbers are 7.09 and 6.52.

Question 8: A number with 2 decimal places is rounded to the nearest whole number. The rounded result is 12. What is the smallest possible number with 2 decimal places it could be?
Answer: 11.50
Working: The smallest decimal with 2 decimal places that rounds up to 12 when rounded to the nearest whole number is 11.50.

--- Rounding decimals to 1 decimal place ---

Question 9: Round 3.42 to 1 decimal place.
Answer: 3.4
Working: The hundredths digit is 2, which is less than 5, so round down to 3.4.

Question 10: Round 7.86 to 1 decimal place.
Answer: 7.9
Working: The hundredths digit is 6, which is 5 or more, so round up to 7.9.

Question 11: Round 12.08 to 1 decimal place.
Answer: 12.1
Working: The hundredths digit is 8, which is 5 or more, so round the tenths digit up from 0 to 1, giving 12.1.

Question 12: A plank of wood has a length of 8.35 metres. Round this length to 1 decimal place.
Answer: 8.4 metres
Working: The hundredths digit is 5, so round up to 8.4 metres.

Question 13: Round 5.97 to 1 decimal place.
Answer: 6.0
Working: The hundredths digit is 7, which is 5 or more. Rounding up increases 9 tenths to 10 tenths, which exchanges to 1 one. The trailing zero is kept to show 1 decimal place, giving 6.0.

Question 14: Round 0.06 to 1 decimal place.
Answer: 0.1
Working: The hundredths digit is 6, which is 5 or more, so round up from 0 tenths to 1 tenth, giving 0.1.

Question 15: A jug contains 1.94 litres of water. Round this capacity to 1 decimal place.
Answer: 1.9 litres
Working: The hundredths digit is 4, which is less than 5, so round down to 1.9 litres.

Question 16: Four pupils record their race times in seconds: 14.28, 14.34, 14.23, and 14.36. Which two times round to 14.3 seconds when rounded to 1 decimal place?
Answer: 14.28 and 14.34
Working: 14.28 rounds up to 14.3 (hundredths digit is 8). 14.34 rounds down to 14.3 (hundredths digit is 4). 14.23 rounds down to 14.2. 14.36 rounds up to 14.4. The two times are 14.28 and 14.34.

Question 17: A number with 2 decimal places is rounded to 1 decimal place. The answer is 4.8. What is the largest possible number with 2 decimal places it could be?
Answer: 4.84
Working: Any number from 4.75 up to 4.84 with 2 decimal places rounds to 4.8. The largest is 4.84.

Question 18: A number with 2 decimal places rounds to 10 when rounded to the nearest whole number, and rounds to 9.9 when rounded to 1 decimal place. Write one possible number that satisfies both conditions.
Answer: 9.87
Working: Numbers that round to 10 as a whole number and 9.9 to 1 decimal place must have tenths digit 8 or 9 such that the whole number rounds up to 10 and the hundredths digit rounds tenths to 9.9. Any value from 9.85 to 9.94 (e.g. 9.85, 9.86, 9.87, 9.88, 9.89, 9.90, 9.91, 9.92, 9.93, 9.94) is valid.
