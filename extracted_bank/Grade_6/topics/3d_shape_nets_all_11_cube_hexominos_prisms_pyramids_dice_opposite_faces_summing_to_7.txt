=== Topic: 3D shape nets (all 11 cube hexominos, prisms, pyramids); dice opposite faces summing to 7 ===
Domain: Geometry: Properties of Shapes

--- All 11 cube hexominos ---

Question 1: A hexomino is a 2D shape made from 6 identical squares joined edge-to-edge. How many of the 35 possible hexominos can fold to form a closed cube?
Answer: 11
Working: There are 35 distinct hexominos in total, and exactly 11 of them can be folded along their seams to form a closed cube.

Question 2: Every net of a cube is made of 6 congruent square faces. How many internal fold lines (seams) join the 6 squares together in any flat cube net?
Answer: 5
Working: To connect 6 separate square faces into a single connected flat net without loops, exactly 5 interior edges (fold lines) are needed (6 faces - 1 = 5 folds).

Question 3: A cube net is arranged in a Latin cross (a 1-4-1 pattern). The middle vertical strip has 4 squares in a column numbered 1, 2, 3, 4 from top to bottom. Two side flaps are attached to square 2. When this net is folded into a cube, which numbered square is opposite square 1?
Answer: 3
Working: In a strip of 4 squares along a column, alternating squares fold to become opposite faces. Therefore, square 1 is opposite square 3, and square 2 is opposite square 4.

Question 4: A pupil tests a hexomino containing 4 squares arranged together in a solid 2 by 2 square block. Explain why this hexomino cannot fold to form a cube.
Answer: Faces would overlap
Working: A 2 by 2 block of 4 squares means 4 faces meet around a single vertex in 2D. When folded into 3D, two of the faces overlap, leaving the cube open.

--- Prism nets ---

Question 5: A right triangular prism is unfolded flat into its net. How many rectangular faces and how many triangular faces does the net contain in total?
Answer: 3 rectangles and 2 triangles
Working: A triangular prism has 2 congruent triangular bases and 3 rectangular lateral faces, giving 3 rectangles and 2 triangles.

Question 6: A right pentagonal prism is unfolded into a flat net. What is the total number of 2D faces in this net?
Answer: 7
Working: A pentagonal prism has 2 pentagonal bases and 5 rectangular lateral faces. Total faces = 2 + 5 = 7.

Question 7: A flat net consists of a central row of 6 identical rectangles, with 1 regular hexagon attached to the top edge and 1 regular hexagon attached to the bottom edge. What 3D shape does this net make when folded?
Answer: Hexagonal prism
Working: A net with 2 hexagonal bases and 6 rectangular side faces folds to make a hexagonal prism.

Question 8: The net of a closed cuboid has 6 rectangular faces. The dimensions of the cuboid are 3 cm by 4 cm by 5 cm. How many pairs of identical congruent rectangular faces make up this net?
Answer: 3
Working: A cuboid has 3 distinct pairs of opposite congruent faces: 3 cm by 4 cm, 3 cm by 5 cm, and 4 cm by 5 cm. That is 3 pairs.

--- Pyramid nets ---

Question 9: The net of a square-based pyramid consists of a square base in the centre. How many triangular faces are attached to the sides of this square base?
Answer: 4
Working: A square has 4 edges, so 4 triangular faces fold up from the edges of the base to meet at the apex.

Question 10: A regular tetrahedron is unfolded into a net made entirely of congruent equilateral triangles. How many triangles are in this net?
Answer: 4
Working: A tetrahedron (triangular-based pyramid) has 4 faces in total, so its net contains 4 congruent equilateral triangles.

Question 11: A pyramid has 7 vertices in total. When unfolded flat, how many triangular faces will be in its net?
Answer: 6
Working: A pyramid with 7 vertices has 1 apex and a 6-sided base (a hexagon). It therefore has 6 triangular lateral faces attached to the hexagonal base.

Question 12: A net is made of 1 regular octagon in the centre with an isosceles triangle attached along each of its 8 outer edges. What is the total number of faces on the 3D solid when the net is folded?
Answer: 9
Working: The net has 1 octagonal base plus 8 triangular lateral faces. Total faces = 1 + 8 = 9 (forming an octagonal pyramid).

--- Dice opposite faces summing to 7 ---

Question 13: On a standard six-sided die, opposite faces always add up to 7. Which number is on the face opposite to 2?
Answer: 5
Working: Opposite faces sum to 7, so 7 - 2 = 5.

Question 14: A standard six-sided die rests on a flat table. The numbers visible on its 5 exposed faces add up to 16. Which number is on the face resting face-down on the table?
Answer: 5
Working: The sum of all 6 faces on a standard die is 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 21. The hidden face is 21 - 16 = 5.

Question 15: A cube net is folded into a standard die where opposite faces sum to 7. The net has a horizontal row of 4 squares numbered 1, 4, 6, 3 from left to right. A top square is attached above 4 and a bottom square is attached below 4. What number must be on the top square if the bottom square shows 2?
Answer: 5
Working: When folded, the top flap and bottom flap fold up to become opposite faces. Since opposite faces must add to 7, the top square must be 7 - 2 = 5.

Question 16: Three standard dice are stacked vertically in a tower on a table. On every die, opposite faces sum to 7. What is the total sum of the numbers on the 3 top faces and the 3 bottom faces of all three dice together?
Answer: 21
Working: Each die contributes a pair of opposite faces (top and bottom), which sum to 7. For 3 dice, the sum of all top and bottom faces is 3 × 7 = 21.

Question 17: Two standard dice are placed side by side so that two faces touch. The face showing on the far left outer end is 3. What number is on the touching face of that first die?
Answer: 4
Working: On the first die, the far left outer face and the touching inner face are opposite each other. Since opposite faces sum to 7, the touching face is 7 - 3 = 4.
