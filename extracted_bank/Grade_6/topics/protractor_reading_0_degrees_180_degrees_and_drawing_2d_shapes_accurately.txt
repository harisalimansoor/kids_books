=== Topic: Protractor reading (0^ degrees--180^ degrees) and drawing 2D shapes accurately ===
Domain: Geometry: Properties of Shapes

--- Protractor reading ---

Question 1: A protractor is placed on an acute angle with its baseline on the bottom ray starting at 0° on the inner scale. The other ray passes through 68° on the inner scale and 112° on the outer scale. What is the size of the acute angle?
Answer: 68°
Working: The baseline starts at 0° on the inner scale, so the angle must be read from the inner scale. The inner scale reads 68°. An acute angle is less than 90°, so the angle is 68°.

Question 2: Sam measures an acute angle with a protractor. He reads the wrong scale and writes down 135°. What is the true measurement of the acute angle in degrees?
Answer: 45°
Working: The inner and outer scales at any point sum to 180°. 180° - 135° = 45°.

Question 3: A protractor is placed over an angle so that its vertex is at the centre mark. One ray points to 24° on the outer scale and the other ray points to 139° on the same outer scale. What is the size of the angle between the two rays?
Answer: 115°
Working: Subtract the smaller scale reading from the larger scale reading: 139° - 24° = 115°.

Question 4: A straight line lies along the baseline of a protractor. An angle of 73° is measured opening from the 0° mark on the right. What is the size of the obtuse angle opening from the 0° mark on the left to the same line?
Answer: 107°
Working: Angles on a straight line add up to 180°. 180° - 73° = 107°.

Question 5: An angle is drawn on a protractor. Ray A points to 38° on the inner scale. Ray B points to 82° on the outer scale. What is the size of the angle between ray A and ray B?
Answer: 60°
Working: Convert Ray B to the inner scale: 180° - 82° = 98°. The angle between Ray A (38°) and Ray B (98°) is 98° - 38° = 60°.

--- Drawing 2D shapes accurately ---

Question 6: A pupil draws a right-angled triangle accurately with a ruler and protractor. One of the acute angles measures 37°. What is the size of the other acute angle in the triangle?
Answer: 53°
Working: Angles in a triangle add up to 180°. The right angle is 90°. 180° - 90° - 37° = 53° (or 90° - 37° = 53°).

Question 7: A pupil accurately draws triangle ABC using a ruler and protractor. Side AB measures 7 cm, angle CAB measures 48°, and angle ABC measures 65°. What must angle BCA measure?
Answer: 67°
Working: The sum of angles in a triangle is 180°. 48° + 65° = 113°. 180° - 113° = 67°.

Question 8: A pupil is drawing an accurate parallelogram with side lengths of 8 cm and 5 cm. One of the interior angles is 64°. What is the size of the adjacent interior obtuse angle?
Answer: 116°
Working: Adjacent angles in a parallelogram add up to 180°. 180° - 64° = 116°.

Question 9: A pupil draws an accurate isosceles triangle where the angle between the two equal sides is 44°. What is the size of each of the two equal base angles?
Answer: 68°
Working: Angles in a triangle sum to 180°. The remaining two equal angles sum to 180° - 44° = 136°. Each base angle is 136° ÷ 2 = 68°.

Question 10: A pupil is constructing a quadrilateral. Three of the interior angles are measured and drawn as 75°, 115°, and 85°. What must the fourth interior angle measure for the shape to close correctly?
Answer: 85°
Working: The sum of interior angles in a quadrilateral is 360°. 75° + 115° + 85° = 275°. 360° - 275° = 85°.
