=== Topic: Long and short division (4-digit ÷ 2-digit); division via factor chains with true remainder scaling; contextual remainder interpretation (rounding up/down) ===
Domain: Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, Division & Operations

--- Long and short division (4-digit ÷ 2-digit) ---

Question 1: Calculate 2,496 ÷ 16.
Answer: 156
Working: 2,496 ÷ 16 = 156 (16 × 100 = 1,600; 16 × 50 = 800; 16 × 6 = 96; 1,600 + 800 + 96 = 2,496).

Question 2: Calculate 4,368 ÷ 24.
Answer: 182
Working: 4,368 ÷ 24 = 182 (24 × 100 = 2,400; 24 × 80 = 1,920; 24 × 2 = 48; 2,400 + 1,920 + 48 = 4,368).

Question 3: Calculate 5,635 ÷ 23.
Answer: 245
Working: 5,635 ÷ 23 = 245 (23 × 200 = 4,600; 23 × 40 = 920; 23 × 5 = 115; 4,600 + 920 + 115 = 5,635).

Question 4: Calculate 8,352 ÷ 29.
Answer: 288
Working: 8,352 ÷ 29 = 288 (29 × 200 = 5,800; 29 × 80 = 2,320; 29 × 8 = 232; 5,800 + 2,320 + 232 = 8,352).

Question 5: Calculate 6,432 ÷ 18. Give your answer as a quotient and a whole-number remainder.
Answer: 357 r 6
Working: 6,432 ÷ 18: 18 × 300 = 5,400; 18 × 50 = 900; 18 × 7 = 126. Total = 6,426. Remainder = 6,432 - 6,426 = 6. Answer is 357 r 6.

Question 6: Calculate 7,548 ÷ 36. Give your answer as a quotient and a whole-number remainder.
Answer: 209 r 24
Working: 7,548 ÷ 36: 36 × 200 = 7,200; 7,548 - 7,200 = 348; 36 × 9 = 324; 348 - 324 = 24. Answer is 209 r 24.

--- Division via factor chains with true remainder scaling ---

Question 7: A pupil calculates 425 ÷ 15 using factors. They divide 425 by 3 to get 141 with a remainder of 2. Then they divide 141 by 5 to get 28 with a remainder of 1. What is the true remainder of 425 ÷ 15?
Answer: 5
Working: True remainder = (second remainder × first divisor) + first remainder = (1 × 3) + 2 = 5.

Question 8: A pupil calculates 550 ÷ 24 by first dividing 550 by 6, which gives 91 with a remainder of 4. They then divide 91 by 4, which gives 22 with a remainder of 3. What is the true remainder of 550 ÷ 24?
Answer: 22
Working: True remainder = (second remainder × first divisor) + first remainder = (3 × 6) + 4 = 18 + 4 = 22.

Question 9: To divide 300 by 21 using factor pairs, 300 is first divided by 3 to give 100 with a remainder of 0. Then 100 is divided by 7 to give 14 with a remainder of 2. What is the true remainder of 300 ÷ 21?
Answer: 6
Working: True remainder = (second remainder × first divisor) + first remainder = (2 × 3) + 0 = 6.

Question 10: Calculate 730 ÷ 18 using the factor chain of dividing by 6 and then by 3. Give your answer as a quotient and the true remainder.
Answer: 40 r 10
Working: 730 ÷ 6 = 121 r 4. 121 ÷ 3 = 40 r 1. True remainder = (1 × 6) + 4 = 10. Quotient is 40. Full answer is 40 r 10.

Question 11: Calculate 865 ÷ 14 using the factor chain of dividing by 2 and then by 7. What is the true remainder?
Answer: 11
Working: 865 ÷ 2 = 432 r 1. 432 ÷ 7 = 61 r 5. True remainder = (5 × 2) + 1 = 10 + 1 = 11.

--- Contextual remainder interpretation (rounding up/down) ---

Question 12: There are 345 pupils and teachers going on a school trip. Each coach seats 36 passengers. How many coaches are needed to transport everyone?
Answer: 10
Working: 345 ÷ 36 = 9 r 21. 9 coaches seat 324 people, leaving 21 people without seats. Therefore, 10 coaches are needed.

Question 13: A baker has 1,450 eggs. A cake recipe requires exactly 24 eggs. How many full cakes can the baker make?
Answer: 60
Working: 1,450 ÷ 24 = 60 r 10. The remaining 10 eggs are not enough for another cake, so the baker can make 60 full cakes.

Question 14: A roll contains 2,500 cm of ribbon. Each gift bow requires 35 cm of ribbon. How many complete bows can be made?
Answer: 71
Working: 2,500 ÷ 35 = 71 r 15. The remaining 15 cm cannot make a complete bow, so 71 complete bows can be made.

Question 15: A farmer harvests 3,250 apples and packs them into boxes of 28. The remaining apples that do not fill a complete box are sold individually at the farm shop. How many apples are sold individually?
Answer: 2
Working: 3,250 ÷ 28: 28 × 116 = 3,248; 3,250 - 3,248 = 2. So 116 full boxes are packed and 2 apples are left over to be sold individually.

Question 16: A hall caretaker has 1,580 chairs to arrange into full rows of 34 chairs. How many complete rows of 34 chairs can be arranged?
Answer: 46
Working: 1,580 ÷ 34: 34 × 46 = 1,564; 1,580 - 1,564 = 16. There are 46 complete rows and 16 spare chairs.
