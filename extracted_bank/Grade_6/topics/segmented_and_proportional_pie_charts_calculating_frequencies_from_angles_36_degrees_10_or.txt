=== Topic: Segmented and proportional pie charts; calculating frequencies from angles (36^ degrees = 10\%) or fractions; cross-chart sample size comparison traps ===
Domain: Statistics

--- Segmented and proportional pie charts ---

Question 1: A pie chart is divided into 12 equal segments to show how 60 children travel to school. Walking takes up 3 of the segments. How many children walk to school?
Answer: 15 children
Working: Each segment represents 60 ÷ 12 = 5 children. 3 segments represent 3 × 5 = 15 children.

Question 2: A pie chart is divided into 20 equal segments to represent a total of 180 visitors to a museum. The sector for children covers 7 of the segments. How many children visited the museum?
Answer: 63 children
Working: Each segment represents 180 ÷ 20 = 9 visitors. 7 segments represent 7 × 9 = 63 children.

Question 3: A pie chart shows the daily activities of a cat over a 24-hour period. The chart is divided into 24 equal segments, with each segment representing 1 hour. Sleeping takes up 14 segments and resting takes up 6 segments. The remaining segments represent eating and playing. How many hours does the cat spend eating and playing?
Answer: 4 hours
Working: Total segments = 24. Sleeping and resting take 14 + 6 = 20 segments. Remaining segments = 24 - 20 = 4 segments. 4 segments = 4 hours.

Question 4: A pie chart divided into 15 equal segments represents the club choices of 450 pupils. Drama takes up 4 segments, Art takes up 5 segments, and Sport takes up the rest. How many more pupils chose Sport than Drama?
Answer: 60 pupils
Working: Each segment represents 450 ÷ 15 = 30 pupils. Sport takes up 15 - (4 + 5) = 6 segments. Difference between Sport and Drama is 6 - 4 = 2 segments. 2 × 30 = 60 pupils.

--- Calculating frequencies from angles or fractions ---

Question 5: In a pie chart representing the favourite sports of 160 children, the sector for swimming is a right angle (90°). How many children chose swimming?
Answer: 40 children
Working: A right angle of 90° represents 90/360 = 1/4 of the total. 1/4 of 160 = 160 ÷ 4 = 40 children.

Question 6: A pie chart represents 250 voters in a local election. In the chart, every 36° represents 10% of the total. Candidate A has a sector with an angle of 72°. How many votes did Candidate A receive?
Answer: 50 votes
Working: 72° = 2 × 36° = 2 × 10% = 20%. 20% of 250 = (250 ÷ 10) × 2 = 50 votes.

Question 7: A pie chart represents the favourite fruit choices of 200 children. The sector for apples has an angle of 108°. How many children chose apples?
Answer: 60 children
Working: 108° ÷ 360° = 108/360 = 3/10 = 30%. 30% of 200 = 3 × 20 = 60 children.

Question 8: A pie chart shows the favourite pets of 480 people. Dogs have a sector angle of 135°, cats have a sector angle of 90°, and rabbits have a sector angle of 45°. The rest of the chart represents other pets. How many people chose other pets?
Answer: 120 people
Working: Sum of known angles = 135° + 90° + 45° = 270°. Angle for other pets = 360° - 270° = 90°. 90°/360° = 1/4 of the total. 1/4 of 480 = 480 ÷ 4 = 120 people.

Question 9: In a survey of 600 people about holiday destinations, the sector for beach holidays has an angle of 144°. How many people chose beach holidays?
Answer: 240 people
Working: 144° ÷ 360° = 144/360 = 4/10 = 40% (or 2/5). 40% of 600 = 4 × 60 = 240 people.

--- Cross-chart sample size comparison traps ---

Question 10: Pie Chart A shows the lunch choices of 40 pupils in Year 5, where 50% chose pizza. Pie Chart B shows the lunch choices of 100 pupils in Year 6, where 25% chose pizza. How many more pupils chose pizza in Year 6 than in Year 5?
Answer: 5 pupils
Working: Year 5: 50% of 40 = 20 pupils. Year 6: 25% of 100 = 25 pupils. Difference = 25 - 20 = 5 pupils.

Question 11: Two schools surveyed pupils about walking to school. School A has 300 pupils, and 40% walk to school. School B has 800 pupils, and 20% walk to school. Which school has more pupils who walk to school, and how many more?
Answer: School B by 40 pupils
Working: School A: 40% of 300 = 120 pupils. School B: 20% of 800 = 160 pupils. School B has more pupils who walk. Difference = 160 - 120 = 40 pupils.

Question 12: Chart X represents the drink choices of 60 people, with a 60° sector for tea. Chart Y represents the drink choices of 300 people, with a 36° sector for tea. How many people chose tea in Chart X, and how many people chose tea in Chart Y?
Answer: Chart X: 10 people, Chart Y: 30 people
Working: Chart X: 60°/360° = 1/6 of 60 = 10 people. Chart Y: 36°/360° = 1/10 of 300 = 30 people.

Question 13: Three schools record how many pupils cycle to school. In School A (200 pupils), 35% cycle. In School B (400 pupils), 20% cycle. In School C (120 pupils), 50% cycle. Order the schools from the fewest pupils who cycle to the most pupils who cycle, stating the number of cycling pupils for each school.
Answer: School C (60 pupils), School A (70 pupils), School B (80 pupils)
Working: School A: 35% of 200 = 70 pupils. School B: 20% of 400 = 80 pupils. School C: 50% of 120 = 60 pupils. Ordered from fewest to most: School C (60), School A (70), School B (80).
