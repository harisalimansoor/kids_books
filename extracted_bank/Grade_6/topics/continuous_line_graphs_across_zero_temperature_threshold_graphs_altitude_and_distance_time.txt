=== Topic: Continuous line graphs across zero (temperature), threshold graphs (altitude), and distance-time graphs (speed, rest plateaus, cumulative distance vs displacement) ===
Domain: Statistics

--- Continuous line graphs across zero (temperature) ---

Question 1: A continuous line graph tracks the temperature in a town every 2 hours: at 02:00 it is -5°C, at 04:00 it is -6°C, at 06:00 it is -2°C, at 08:00 it is 3°C, and at 10:00 it is 7°C. What is the total rise in temperature between the coldest time recorded and 10:00?
Answer: 13°C
Working: Coldest temperature is -6°C at 04:00. Temperature at 10:00 is 7°C. Rise = 7 - (-6) = 7 + 6 = 13°C.

Question 2: A line graph shows the hourly temperature outside a school: 06:00 (-4°C), 07:00 (-2°C), 08:00 (-1°C), 09:00 (2°C), 10:00 (5°C). Between which two consecutive hours did the temperature cross zero degrees Celsius?
Answer: 08:00 and 09:00
Working: At 08:00 the temperature was -1°C (below zero) and at 09:00 it was 2°C (above zero). Therefore, the line crossed 0°C between 08:00 and 09:00.

Question 3: A weather station line graph shows temperatures recorded on a mountain: 20:00 (4°C), 23:00 (1°C), 02:00 (-3°C), 05:00 (-7°C), 08:00 (-2°C), 11:00 (6°C). What is the difference in degrees Celsius between the highest temperature and the lowest temperature recorded?
Answer: 13°C
Working: Highest temperature = 6°C (at 11:00). Lowest temperature = -7°C (at 05:00). Difference = 6 - (-7) = 13°C.

Question 4: A science experiment tracks the temperature of a liquid cooling and then freezing. The line graph shows: 0 min (8°C), 5 min (3°C), 10 min (0°C), 15 min (-4°C), 20 min (-9°C). How many degrees Celsius did the temperature fall between 5 minutes and 20 minutes?
Answer: 12°C
Working: Temperature at 5 min = 3°C. Temperature at 20 min = -9°C. Total fall = 3 - (-9) = 12°C.

--- Threshold graphs (altitude) ---

Question 5: A line graph tracks a hill walker's altitude above sea level: Start (150 m), 2 km (320 m), 4 km (480 m), 6 km (650 m), 8 km (820 m). The cloud base threshold begins at 500 m. How many metres of vertical ascent did the walker make from the 500 m threshold to the 8 km checkpoint?
Answer: 320 m
Working: Altitude at 8 km = 820 m. Cloud threshold = 500 m. Remaining ascent = 820 - 500 = 320 m.

Question 6: A drone flight graph records altitude at 1-minute intervals: 1 min (45 m), 2 min (90 m), 3 min (125 m), 4 min (140 m), 5 min (115 m), 6 min (80 m). The safety threshold limit is 100 m. At how many of these 6 recorded checkpoints was the drone flying above the 100 m threshold?
Answer: 3
Working: Check the altitudes against 100 m: 45 m (<100), 90 m (<100), 125 m (>100), 140 m (>100), 115 m (>100), 80 m (<100). There are 3 checkpoints above 100 m (minutes 3, 4, and 5).

Question 7: A research submarine dive is shown on a depth graph relative to sea level: 0 min (0 m), 10 min (-40 m), 20 min (-85 m), 30 min (-85 m), 40 min (-50 m), 50 min (0 m). The deep-pressure threshold begins below -60 m. The graph shows the submarine crossed below -60 m at 15 minutes and rose back above -60 m at 36 minutes. For how many minutes was the submarine at or below the -60 m threshold?
Answer: 21 minutes
Working: Entry time at threshold = 15 min. Exit time at threshold = 36 min. Duration = 36 - 15 = 21 minutes.

Question 8: An aircraft climb graph plots altitude against time: 0 min (0 m), 4 min (1,200 m), 8 min (2,600 m), 12 min (4,100 m), 16 min (5,500 m). The minimum cruising altitude threshold is 5,000 m. How many metres did the aircraft climb between 8 minutes and 16 minutes?
Answer: 2,900 m
Working: Altitude at 8 min = 2,600 m. Altitude at 16 min = 5,500 m. Climb = 5,500 - 2,600 = 2,900 m.

--- Distance-time graphs (speed, rest plateaus, cumulative distance vs displacement) ---

Question 9: A distance-time graph shows a cyclist's journey: from 10:00 to 11:00 the cyclist travels 15 km; from 11:00 to 11:45 the graph shows a horizontal line plateau at 15 km; from 11:45 to 12:45 the cyclist travels to 30 km. For how many minutes did the cyclist rest at the plateau?
Answer: 45 minutes
Working: The horizontal line plateau represents resting from 11:00 to 11:45, which is 45 minutes.

Question 10: A displacement-time graph shows Oliver walking from home to the library and back. From 0 to 30 minutes, he walks 2 km to the library. From 30 to 50 minutes, he stays at the library (distance from home remains 2 km). From 50 to 80 minutes, he walks straight back home (distance decreases to 0 km). What is the total cumulative distance Oliver walked during the 80 minutes?
Answer: 4 km
Working: Distance to library = 2 km. Distance back home = 2 km. Total cumulative distance = 2 + 2 = 4 km.

Question 11: A distance-time graph for a coach trip shows that between 09:00 and 09:30 the coach travelled from 0 km to 24 km at a constant speed. What was the speed of the coach during this 30-minute period in kilometres per hour (km/h)?
Answer: 48 km/h
Working: Distance = 24 km in 30 minutes (0.5 hours). Speed = 24 ÷ 0.5 = 48 km/h (or 24 × 2 = 48 km/h).

Question 12: A runner's distance-time graph shows: 0 to 20 minutes (covers 0 km to 3 km); 20 to 30 minutes (rest plateau at 3 km); 30 to 50 minutes (covers 3 km to 8 km). What was the runner's speed in kilometres per hour (km/h) during the final 20-minute section from 30 to 50 minutes?
Answer: 15 km/h
Working: Distance in final section = 8 - 3 = 5 km. Time taken = 20 minutes = 1/3 of an hour. Speed = 5 × 3 = 15 km/h.
