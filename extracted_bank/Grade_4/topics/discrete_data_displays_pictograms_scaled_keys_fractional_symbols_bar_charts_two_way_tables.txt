=== Topic: Discrete data displays: pictograms (scaled keys, fractional symbols), bar charts, two-way tables ===
Domain: Statistics

--- Pictograms (scaled keys, fractional symbols) ---

Question 1: In a pictogram showing the number of books read by different classes, each symbol of an open book represents 8 books. Class 4B has 4 whole book symbols and 1 half book symbol. How many books did Class 4B read?
Answer: 36
Working: 1 whole symbol = 8 books. 4 whole symbols = 4 × 8 = 32 books. 1 half symbol = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 books. Total = 32 + 4 = 36 books.

Question 2: A pictogram shows the number of visitors to a wildlife park. The key shows that 1 full circle represents 12 visitors. On Friday, the pictogram shows 3 whole circles and 1 quarter circle. How many visitors were there on Friday?
Answer: 39
Working: 1 whole circle = 12 visitors. 3 whole circles = 3 × 12 = 36 visitors. 1 quarter circle = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 visitors. Total = 36 + 3 = 39 visitors.

Question 3: In a school survey about favourite fruits, 1 apple symbol represents 8 children. Apples: 3 whole symbols and 1 half symbol. Bananas: 5 whole symbols and 1 quarter symbol. How many more children chose bananas than apples?
Answer: 14
Working: Children who chose apples = (3 × 8) + (8 ÷ 2) = 24 + 4 = 28. Children who chose bananas = (5 × 8) + (8 ÷ 4) = 40 + 2 = 42. Difference = 42 - 28 = 14.

Question 4: A pictogram uses 1 star to represent 6 reward points. Team Oak has 5 whole stars and 1 half star. Team Ash has 4 whole stars. What is the total number of reward points scored by both teams together?
Answer: 57
Working: Team Oak: (5 × 6) + 3 = 30 + 3 = 33 points. Team Ash: 4 × 6 = 24 points. Total = 33 + 24 = 57 points.

--- Bar charts ---

Question 5: A bar chart shows the number of pets owned by Year 4 pupils. The vertical axis goes up in intervals of 5. The bar for Dogs reaches 25, Cats reaches 20, Rabbits reaches 10, and Hamsters reaches 5. How many more pupils have dogs than hamsters?
Answer: 20
Working: Number of dogs = 25. Number of hamsters = 5. Difference = 25 - 5 = 20.

Question 6: A bar chart displays the number of packed lunches ordered across four days. The vertical scale goes up in steps of 10. Monday shows 40 lunches, Tuesday shows 65 lunches (halfway between 60 and 70), Wednesday shows 50 lunches, and Thursday shows 55 lunches (halfway between 50 and 60). How many packed lunches were ordered in total across all four days?
Answer: 210
Working: Monday = 40, Tuesday = 65, Wednesday = 50, Thursday = 55. Total = 40 + 65 + 50 + 55 = 210 lunches.

Question 7: A bar chart shows the favourite sports of 100 pupils. The vertical scale has intervals of 4. Football has 36 votes, Swimming has 24 votes, and Netball has 18 votes. The rest of the pupils voted for Running. How many pupils voted for Running?
Answer: 22
Working: Votes for Football, Swimming, and Netball = 36 + 24 + 18 = 78. Total pupils = 100. Pupils voting for Running = 100 - 78 = 22.

Question 8: A bar chart shows the number of vehicles passing a school between 8:00 am and 9:00 am. Cars: 48, Bicycles: 16, Buses: 8, Vans: 22. What is the difference between the number of cars and the combined total of bicycles and vans?
Answer: 10
Working: Combined total of bicycles and vans = 16 + 22 = 38. Number of cars = 48. Difference = 48 - 38 = 10.

--- Two-way tables ---

Question 9: A two-way table shows the after-school clubs attended by Year 4 pupils.

- Art Club: 12 boys, 15 girls
- Chess Club: 9 boys, 6 girls
- Drama Club: 8 boys, 14 girls

How many children attend Art Club and Drama Club altogether?
Answer: 49
Working: Art Club total = 12 + 15 = 27. Drama Club total = 8 + 14 = 22. Combined total = 27 + 22 = 49 children.

Question 10: The two-way table shows how 60 children travel to school.

- Walk: 14 boys, 18 girls (Total = 32)
- Bus: 8 boys, [missing number] girls
- Car: 6 boys, 5 girls (Total = 11)

There are 60 children in total. How many girls travel to school by bus?
Answer: 9
Working: Known totals: Walk = 32, Car = 11. Total for Walk and Car = 32 + 11 = 43. Total traveling by bus = 60 - 43 = 17. Number of boys on bus = 8. Girls on bus = 17 - 8 = 9.

Question 11: A two-way table shows choices of school lunches on Friday:

- Pizza: 24 boys, 28 girls
- Pasta: 16 boys, 19 girls
- Jacket Potato: 7 boys, 11 girls

How many more girls than boys chose Pizza or Pasta?
Answer: 7
Working: Girls choosing Pizza or Pasta = 28 + 19 = 47. Boys choosing Pizza or Pasta = 24 + 16 = 40. Difference = 47 - 40 = 7.

Question 12: A sports coach records whether 50 pupils can swim 25 metres.

- Year 3: 16 can swim, 9 cannot swim (Total = 25)
- Year 4: 21 can swim, [missing number] cannot swim (Total = 25)

How many pupils in total across Year 3 and Year 4 cannot swim 25 metres?
Answer: 13
Working: Year 4 pupils who cannot swim = 25 - 21 = 4. Year 3 pupils who cannot swim = 9. Total pupils who cannot swim = 9 + 4 = 13.
