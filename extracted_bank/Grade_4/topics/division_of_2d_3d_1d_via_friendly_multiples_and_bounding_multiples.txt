=== Topic: Division of 2D/3D ÷ 1D via friendly multiples and bounding multiples ===
Domain: Multiplication and Division

--- Friendly multiples ---

Question 1: Use friendly multiples to calculate $78 \div 6$.
Partition 78 into $60 + 18$.
What is the value of $(60 \div 6) + (18 \div 6)$?
Answer: 13
Working: 60 ÷ 6 = 10
18 ÷ 6 = 3
10 + 3 = 13

Question 2: Calculate $96 \div 4$ by partitioning 96 into two friendly multiples of 4: 80 and 16.
Answer: 24
Working: 96 = 80 + 16
80 ÷ 4 = 20
16 ÷ 4 = 4
20 + 4 = 24

Question 3: Calculate $168 \div 6$ by partitioning 168 into two friendly multiples of 6 ($120 + 48$).
Answer: 28
Working: 168 = 120 + 48
120 ÷ 6 = 20
48 ÷ 6 = 8
20 + 8 = 28

Question 4: A baker packs 112 muffins into boxes of 8. By partitioning 112 into friendly multiples $80 + 32$, work out how many boxes of muffins the baker fills.
Answer: 14
Working: 112 = 80 + 32
80 ÷ 8 = 10
32 ÷ 8 = 4
10 + 4 = 14 boxes

Question 5: To calculate $224 \div 7$, partition 224 into $210 + 14$. What is the quotient?
Answer: 32
Working: 224 = 210 + 14
210 ÷ 7 = 30
14 ÷ 7 = 2
30 + 2 = 32

Question 6: Sita partitions 175 into 150 and another friendly multiple of 5 to solve $175 \div 5$. What is the missing number in her partition, and what is the final answer?
Answer: 25 and 35
Working: 175 - 150 = 25
150 ÷ 5 = 30
25 ÷ 5 = 5
30 + 5 = 35

--- Bounding multiples ---

Question 7: Look at these multiplication facts:
$6 \times 20 = 120$
$6 \times 30 = 180$

Between which two multiples of 10 does the answer to $138 \div 6$ lie?
Answer: 20 and 30
Working: 138 is between 120 and 180.
120 = 6 × 20 and 180 = 6 × 30.
Therefore, 138 ÷ 6 lies between 20 and 30.

Question 8: Write the two bounding multiples of 10 for the calculation $192 \div 8$.
$8 \times 20 = 160$
$8 \times 30 = 240$
Between which two tens does the quotient of $192 \div 8$ lie?
Answer: 20 and 30
Working: 160 < 192 < 240
8 × 20 = 160 and 8 × 30 = 240
The quotient lies between 20 and 30.

Question 9: Find the two multiples of 70 (multiples of 10 times 7) that bound the number 364.
Answer: 350 and 420
Working: 7 × 50 = 350
7 × 60 = 420
350 < 364 < 420

Question 10: To estimate $264 \div 8$, find the two multiples of 10 that bound the quotient by calculating $8 \times 30$ and $8 \times 40$. State between which two multiples of 10 the quotient lies.
Answer: 30 and 40
Working: 8 × 30 = 240
8 × 40 = 320
240 < 264 < 320, so the quotient lies between 30 and 40.

Question 11: A school shares 336 exercise books equally among 6 year groups.
a) Which two multiples of 10 times 6 bound 336 ($6 \times 50$ and $6 \times 60$)?
b) Using friendly multiples, calculate the exact number of books each year group receives.
Answer: 300 and 360; 56
Working: a) 6 × 50 = 300 and 6 × 60 = 360, so 300 and 360 bound 336.
b) Partition 336 into 300 + 36: (300 ÷ 6) + (36 ÷ 6) = 50 + 6 = 56 books.
