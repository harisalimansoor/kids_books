=== Topic: Comparing and ordering unit fractions and fractions with the same denominator or same numerator ===
Domain: Fractions

--- Comparing and ordering unit fractions ---

Question 1: Which is the greater fraction: 1/3 or 1/5?
Answer: 1/3
Working: When unit fractions are compared, a smaller denominator means the whole is split into fewer, larger parts. Since 3 is smaller than 5, 1/3 is greater than 1/5.

Question 2: Write < or > to make the statement true: 1/8 [ ] 1/4.
Answer: <
Working: An eighth is smaller than a quarter because the whole is divided into 8 equal parts instead of 4 equal parts. Therefore, 1/8 < 1/4.

Question 3: Write these unit fractions in order from smallest to greatest: 1/3, 1/10, 1/6, 1/2.
Answer: 1/10, 1/6, 1/3, 1/2
Working: The larger the denominator, the smaller each unit fraction. Ordering the denominators from largest to smallest gives 10, 6, 3, 2. So the fractions from smallest to greatest are 1/10, 1/6, 1/3, 1/2.

Question 4: Write these unit fractions in order from greatest to smallest: 1/5, 1/2, 1/8, 1/4.
Answer: 1/2, 1/4, 1/5, 1/8
Working: The smaller the denominator, the larger each unit fraction. Ordering the denominators from smallest to largest gives 2, 4, 5, 8. So the fractions from greatest to smallest are 1/2, 1/4, 1/5, 1/8.

Question 5: Ella drinks 1/4 of a litre of water. Oliver drinks 1/6 of a litre of water. Who drinks more water?
Answer: Ella
Working: Quarters are larger than sixths because the whole is split into fewer equal parts. 1/4 is greater than 1/6, so Ella drinks more water.

Question 6: A bakery makes two identical loaves of bread. Loaf A is sliced into 6 equal pieces and Loaf B is sliced into 8 equal pieces. Which loaf has the larger slices, Loaf A or Loaf B?
Answer: Loaf A
Working: Each slice of Loaf A is 1/6 of the loaf and each slice of Loaf B is 1/8 of the loaf. Dividing into 6 parts gives larger pieces than dividing into 8 parts (1/6 > 1/8), so Loaf A has the larger slices.

--- Comparing and ordering fractions with the same denominator ---

Question 7: Which is the smaller fraction: 2/5 or 4/5?
Answer: 2/5
Working: Both fractions have the same denominator (5). Comparing the numerators, 2 is smaller than 4, so 2/5 is smaller than 4/5.

Question 8: Write < or > to make the statement true: 7/10 [ ] 3/10.
Answer: >
Working: Both fractions represent parts of tenths. 7 tenths is greater than 3 tenths, so 7/10 > 3/10.

Question 9: Write these fractions in order from smallest to greatest: 5/8, 1/8, 7/8, 3/8.
Answer: 1/8, 3/8, 5/8, 7/8
Working: The denominators are all 8. Order the numerators from smallest to greatest: 1, 3, 5, 7. So the fractions in order are 1/8, 3/8, 5/8, 7/8.

Question 10: Write these fractions in order from greatest to smallest: 4/6, 1/6, 5/6, 2/6.
Answer: 5/6, 4/6, 2/6, 1/6
Working: The denominators are all 6. Order the numerators from greatest to smallest: 5, 4, 2, 1. So the fractions in order are 5/6, 4/6, 2/6, 1/6.

Question 11: Harry has eaten 3/10 of his chocolate bar. Chloe has eaten 6/10 of her identical chocolate bar. Who has eaten the smaller amount of chocolate?
Answer: Harry
Working: Both chocolate bars are split into tenths. Comparing 3/10 and 6/10, 3 is less than 6. Harry has eaten the smaller amount.

Question 12: Three children run parts of a track. Liam runs 4/7 of the track, Sophie runs 2/7 of the track, and Noah runs 6/7 of the track. Write the names of the children in order from the shortest distance run to the longest distance run.
Answer: Sophie, Liam, Noah
Working: Compare the fractions with denominator 7: 2/7 < 4/7 < 6/7. Sophie ran 2/7, Liam ran 4/7, and Noah ran 6/7. In order from shortest to longest distance: Sophie, Liam, Noah.

--- Comparing and ordering fractions with the same numerator ---

Question 13: Which is the greater fraction: 2/3 or 2/5?
Answer: 2/3
Working: Both fractions have the same numerator (2). Thirds are larger parts than fifths, so 2 thirds is greater than 2 fifths (2/3 > 2/5).

Question 14: Write < or > to make the statement true: 3/8 [ ] 3/4.
Answer: <
Working: Both fractions have a numerator of 3. Eighths are smaller parts than quarters because the denominator is larger. Therefore, 3/8 < 3/4.

Question 15: Write these fractions in order from smallest to greatest: 2/5, 2/10, 2/3, 2/6.
Answer: 2/10, 2/6, 2/5, 2/3
Working: All fractions have a numerator of 2. When numerators are equal, the fraction with the largest denominator has the smallest parts. Ordering denominators from largest to smallest gives 10, 6, 5, 3. The fractions in order from smallest to greatest are 2/10, 2/6, 2/5, 2/3.

Question 16: Write these fractions in order from greatest to smallest: 3/4, 3/10, 3/5, 3/8.
Answer: 3/4, 3/5, 3/8, 3/10
Working: All fractions have a numerator of 3. The smaller the denominator, the larger each part. Ordering denominators from smallest to largest gives 4, 5, 8, 10. The fractions in order from greatest to smallest are 3/4, 3/5, 3/8, 3/10.

Question 17: Amira paints 4/5 of a fence. George paints 4/8 of an identical fence. Who has painted the larger part of their fence?
Answer: Amira
Working: Both have painted 4 equal parts, but fifths are larger than eighths because 5 is smaller than 8. 4/5 is greater than 4/8, so Amira painted the larger part.

Question 18: Three identical ribbons are each 1 metre long. Ribbon A has 3/10 used, Ribbon B has 3/4 used, and Ribbon C has 3/5 used. List the ribbons in order from the shortest length used to the longest length used.
Answer: Ribbon A, Ribbon C, Ribbon B
Working: All three fractions have a numerator of 3. Compare denominators to order fractions from smallest to greatest: 10 > 5 > 4 means 3/10 < 3/5 < 3/4. The ribbons in order from shortest length used to longest length used are Ribbon A, Ribbon C, Ribbon B.
