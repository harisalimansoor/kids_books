=== Topic: Time duration calculations (start, finish, elapsed time across hours), a.m./p.m. conventions, and calendar units (days in months, leap years) ===
Domain: Measurement

--- Time duration calculations (start, finish, elapsed time across hours) ---

Question 1: A swimming lesson starts at 4:15 pm and ends at 4:55 pm. How many minutes does the lesson last?
Answer: 40 minutes
Working: Count from 15 minutes past the hour to 55 minutes past the hour: 55 - 15 = 40 minutes.

Question 2: A cartoon starts at 3:40 pm and lasts for 35 minutes. What time does the cartoon finish?
Answer: 4:15 pm
Working: From 3:40 pm, add 20 minutes to reach 4:00 pm. Then add the remaining 15 minutes to reach 4:15 pm.

Question 3: A bus journey takes 45 minutes. The bus arrives at the station at 11:10 am. What time did the bus start its journey?
Answer: 10:25 am
Working: Count back 10 minutes from 11:10 am to reach 11:00 am. Then count back the remaining 35 minutes: 60 - 35 = 25 minutes past 10, which is 10:25 am.

Question 4: A football match kicks off at 10:45 am and finishes at 12:15 pm. How many minutes does the match last in total?
Answer: 90 minutes
Working: From 10:45 am to 11:00 am is 15 minutes. From 11:00 am to 12:00 pm is 60 minutes. From 12:00 pm to 12:15 pm is 15 minutes. Total time: 15 + 60 + 15 = 90 minutes.

Question 5: Maya practised the piano from 2:50 pm until 3:25 pm. Liam practised the violin from 4:35 pm until 5:15 pm. Who practised for longer, and by how many minutes?
Answer: Liam by 5 minutes
Working: Maya: 2:50 pm to 3:00 pm is 10 minutes, plus 25 minutes to 3:25 pm = 35 minutes. Liam: 4:35 pm to 5:00 pm is 25 minutes, plus 15 minutes to 5:15 pm = 40 minutes. 40 - 35 = 5 minutes. Liam practised for longer by 5 minutes.

Question 6: A cake is put into the oven at 11:35 am. It bakes for 40 minutes. What time should the cake be taken out of the oven?
Answer: 12:15 pm
Working: From 11:35 am, add 25 minutes to reach 12:00 pm (noon). 40 - 25 = 15 minutes remaining. 12:00 pm + 15 minutes = 12:15 pm.

--- a.m./p.m. conventions ---

Question 7: Sam eats his breakfast at half past 7 in the morning. Write this time using digits and either am or pm.
Answer: 7:30 am
Working: Half past 7 is written as 7:30. Because it is in the morning, use am: 7:30 am.

Question 8: An evening football match starts at quarter past 6. Write this time using digits and either am or pm.
Answer: 6:15 pm
Working: Quarter past 6 is written as 6:15. Because it is in the evening, use pm: 6:15 pm.

Question 9: A school trip departs at 8:45 am and returns at 3:15 pm on the same day. Does the school trip return in the morning or the afternoon?
Answer: Afternoon
Working: The time 3:15 pm is after 12:00 noon, which means it is in the afternoon.

Question 10: On the same day, Event A takes place at 11:30 am and Event B takes place at 1:15 pm. Which event takes place earlier?
Answer: Event A
Working: 11:30 am is in the morning before noon, while 1:15 pm is in the afternoon after noon. Therefore, Event A takes place earlier.

--- Calendar units (days in months, leap years) ---

Question 11: How many days are there in the month of April?
Answer: 30
Working: April is one of the four months with 30 days (April, June, September, November).

Question 12: How many days are in a standard, non-leap year?
Answer: 365
Working: A normal year has 365 days.

Question 13: How many days does the month of February have during a leap year?
Answer: 29
Working: In a non-leap year February has 28 days, but in a leap year it has 1 extra day, making 29 days.

Question 14: How many days are there altogether in July and August?
Answer: 62
Working: July has 31 days. August has 31 days. 31 + 31 = 62 days.

Question 15: A leap year occurs once every 4 years. If the year 2020 was a leap year, what was the next leap year?
Answer: 2024
Working: Add 4 years to the previous leap year: 2020 + 4 = 2024.

Question 16: How many days are there altogether in the months of September and October?
Answer: 61
Working: September has 30 days. October has 31 days. 30 + 31 = 61 days.
