=== Topic: Drawing 2-D shapes, making 3-D shapes, and describing geometric attributes ===
Domain: Geometry (Properties of Shapes

--- Drawing 2-D shapes ---

Question 1: Sam uses a ruler to draw a closed 2-D shape with 5 straight sides. What is the mathematical name of this 2-D shape?
Answer: pentagon
Working: A closed 2-D shape with 5 straight sides is called a pentagon.

Question 2: Ava draws a shape that has 6 straight sides and 6 vertices. What is the name of the polygon she has drawn?
Answer: hexagon
Working: A polygon with 6 straight sides and 6 vertices is a hexagon.

Question 3: Liam wants to draw an octagon on squared paper. How many straight sides does he need to draw?
Answer: 8
Working: An octagon is an 8-sided polygon, so he needs to draw 8 straight sides.

Question 4: Maya draws a triangle on dot paper. Two of the sides meet at a right angle. What special type of triangle has Maya drawn?
Answer: right-angled triangle
Working: A triangle that contains one right angle is called a right-angled triangle.

Question 5: Oliver draws a quadrilateral on grid paper with 4 straight sides of equal length and 4 right angles. Which 2-D shape has he drawn?
Answer: square
Working: A 4-sided shape with 4 equal sides and 4 right angles is a square.

Question 6: Ella draws a 4-sided polygon that has 2 pairs of parallel sides and no right angles. All 4 sides are equal in length. What is the name of this shape?
Answer: rhombus
Working: A 4-sided shape with all sides equal in length and opposite sides parallel, but no right angles, is a rhombus.

--- Making 3-D shapes ---

Question 7: Leo is making the frame of a cube using straight straws for the edges and small balls of modelling clay for the vertices. How many straws does he need in total?
Answer: 12
Working: A cube has 12 edges, so Leo needs 12 straws.

Question 8: Priya is building the skeleton of a square-based pyramid using straws for edges and clay balls for corners. How many balls of clay does she need for the vertices?
Answer: 5
Working: A square-based pyramid has 4 base vertices and 1 apex vertex, which is 5 vertices in total. She needs 5 balls of clay.

Question 9: Noah makes a 3-D shape using 6 identical square card faces taped together. What 3-D shape has Noah made?
Answer: cube
Working: A 3-D shape formed by 6 identical square faces is a cube.

Question 10: Holly uses modelling sticks and clay to make a triangular prism. How many sticks does she need for all the edges?
Answer: 9
Working: A triangular prism has 9 edges (3 on each triangular face and 3 joining edges), so Holly needs 9 sticks.

Question 11: Jack makes a 3-D shape out of flat card. He uses 2 circular faces and 1 curved rectangular face. Which 3-D shape did Jack make?
Answer: cylinder
Working: A 3-D shape with 2 identical circular faces and 1 curved surface connecting them is a cylinder.

Question 12: A pupil builds a model of a triangular pyramid (tetrahedron) using modelling clay balls for the vertices and straws for the edges. How many clay balls and how many straws are needed in total?
Answer: 4 clay balls and 6 straws
Working: A triangular pyramid has 4 vertices and 6 edges. Therefore, 4 clay balls and 6 straws are needed.

--- Describing geometric attributes ---

Question 13: How many flat faces does a cuboid have?
Answer: 6
Working: A cuboid has 6 flat rectangular faces.

Question 14: How many vertices does a triangular prism have?
Answer: 6
Working: A triangular prism has 2 triangular bases with 3 vertices each, giving 3 + 3 = 6 vertices in total.

Question 15: A 3-D shape has 1 curved surface, 1 flat circular face, and 1 vertex at the top. What is the name of this shape?
Answer: cone
Working: A cone has 1 flat circular face, 1 curved surface, and 1 apex/vertex.

Question 16: How many straight edges does a square-based pyramid have?
Answer: 8
Working: A square-based pyramid has 4 edges around the square base and 4 sloping edges connecting to the apex: 4 + 4 = 8 edges.

Question 17: A 3-D shape is turned upside down. It still has 5 faces: 2 triangular faces and 3 rectangular faces. What is the name of this shape in this new orientation?
Answer: triangular prism
Working: Changing the orientation of a 3-D shape does not change what shape it is. A shape with 2 triangular faces and 3 rectangular faces is always a triangular prism.

Question 18: A 3-D shape has 0 flat faces, 0 straight edges, and 0 vertices. It is completely round. What is the name of this 3-D shape?
Answer: sphere
Working: A shape with 1 continuous curved surface and no flat faces, edges, or vertices is a sphere.

Question 19: Look at these two 3-D shapes: a cube and a square-based pyramid. How many more edges does the cube have than the square-based pyramid?
Answer: 4
Working: A cube has 12 edges. A square-based pyramid has 8 edges. 12 - 8 = 4 more edges.
