=== Topic: Measuring, comparing, adding, and subtracting capacity/volume (l/ml) and scale reading ===
Domain: Measurement

--- Measuring capacity and volume ---

Question 1: How many millilitres are there in 1 litre?
Answer: 1000 ml
Working: 1 litre is equal to 1000 millilitres.

Question 2: A bottle contains half a litre of water. How many millilitres of water is this?
Answer: 500 ml
Working: 1 litre = 1000 ml. Half of 1000 ml = 1000 ÷ 2 = 500 ml.

Question 3: A recipe needs one quarter of a litre of milk. How many millilitres of milk is needed?
Answer: 250 ml
Working: 1 litre = 1000 ml. One quarter of 1000 ml = 1000 ÷ 4 = 250 ml.

Question 4: Four identical cups each hold 150 ml of juice. What is the total volume of juice in all four cups?
Answer: 600 ml
Working: 4 × 150 ml = 600 ml.

Question 5: A jug has a capacity of 1 litre. It currently contains 650 ml of water. How many more millilitres are needed to fill the jug completely?
Answer: 350 ml
Working: 1 litre = 1000 ml. 1000 ml - 650 ml = 350 ml.

--- Comparing capacity and volume ---

Question 6: A red bottle holds 450 ml of water and a blue bottle holds 520 ml of water. Which bottle holds more water?
Answer: blue bottle
Working: 520 ml is greater than 450 ml, so the blue bottle holds more water.

Question 7: A bucket has a capacity of 9 litres. A watering can has a capacity of 4 litres. How many fewer litres does the watering can hold than the bucket?
Answer: 5 litres
Working: 9 litres - 4 litres = 5 litres.

Question 8: Container A holds 340 ml, Container B holds 304 ml, and Container C holds 430 ml. Which container has the smallest capacity?
Answer: Container B
Working: Comparing 340 ml, 304 ml, and 430 ml: 304 ml is the smallest value.

Question 9: A flask contains 750 ml of soup. A mug contains 280 ml of soup. How many more millilitres of soup are in the flask than in the mug?
Answer: 470 ml
Working: 750 ml - 280 ml = 470 ml.

Question 10: Write these capacities in order from smallest to greatest: 650 ml, 560 ml, 506 ml.
Answer: 506 ml, 560 ml, 650 ml
Working: 506 ml < 560 ml < 650 ml.

--- Adding and subtracting capacity and volume ---

Question 11: A chef mixes 230 ml of milk with 140 ml of water. What is the total volume of liquid?
Answer: 370 ml
Working: 230 ml + 140 ml = 370 ml.

Question 12: A barrel holds 450 litres of rainwater. 175 litres of water are used for the garden. How many litres of water are left in the barrel?
Answer: 275 litres
Working: 450 litres - 175 litres = 275 litres.

Question 13: A painter mixes 348 ml of blue paint and 285 ml of yellow paint. What is the total volume of paint mixed?
Answer: 633 ml
Working: 348 ml + 285 ml = 633 ml.

Question 14: A jug holds 850 ml of orange juice. Leo pours out two glasses of juice. Each glass contains 220 ml. How many millilitres of juice remain in the jug?
Answer: 410 ml
Working: Total poured out = 220 ml + 220 ml = 440 ml. Juice left = 850 ml - 440 ml = 410 ml.

Question 15: A fish tank contains 265 litres of water. Priya adds 148 litres of water to it, and then removes 75 litres. How many litres of water are in the tank now?
Answer: 338 litres
Working: 265 + 148 = 413 litres. 413 - 75 = 338 litres.

--- Scale reading ---

Question 16: A measuring cylinder has numbered marks at every 100 ml. Between 0 ml and 100 ml, there are 2 equal divisions. How many millilitres does each division represent?
Answer: 50 ml
Working: 100 ml ÷ 2 = 50 ml.

Question 17: A measuring jug has marks every 100 ml from 0 ml to 1000 ml. The water level is exactly halfway between 300 ml and 400 ml. What is the volume of water in the jug?
Answer: 350 ml
Working: Halfway between 300 ml and 400 ml is 300 + 50 = 350 ml.

Question 18: A measuring cylinder has numbered marks at 0 ml, 100 ml, 200 ml, and so on. There are 5 equal divisions between 0 ml and 100 ml. What volume is represented by each small division?
Answer: 20 ml
Working: 100 ml ÷ 5 = 20 ml.

Question 19: A scale on a measuring cylinder has marked intervals of 50 ml. The liquid level starts at 0 ml and reaches 7 intervals high. What is the volume of liquid in the cylinder?
Answer: 350 ml
Working: 7 × 50 ml = 350 ml.

Question 20: A measuring jug has numbered marks at every 200 ml with 4 equal intervals between each numbered mark. The water level is at 3 intervals above 400 ml. What is the volume of water?
Answer: 550 ml
Working: Each interval = 200 ml ÷ 4 = 50 ml. 3 intervals above 400 ml = 400 ml + (3 × 50 ml) = 400 ml + 150 ml = 550 ml.
