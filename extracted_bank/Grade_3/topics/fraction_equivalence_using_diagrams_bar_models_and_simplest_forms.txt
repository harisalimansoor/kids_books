=== Topic: Fraction equivalence using diagrams, bar models, and simplest forms ===
Domain: Fractions

--- Fraction equivalence using diagrams ---

Question 1: A circle is divided into 2 equal parts and 1 part is shaded to show 1/2. An identical circle is divided into 4 equal parts. How many parts must be shaded to show the same fraction?
Answer: 2
Working: 1/2 is equivalent to 2/4, so 2 parts out of 4 must be shaded.

Question 2: A square diagram is divided into 3 equal columns, and 1 column is shaded. Another identical square is divided into 6 equal parts. How many parts must be shaded so that the fraction shaded is equivalent to 1/3?
Answer: 2
Working: Each third is split into 2 sixths. 1/3 is equivalent to 2/6, so 2 parts must be shaded.

Question 3: A rectangle is divided into 8 equal parts. 4 of the parts are shaded. What fraction with a denominator of 2 is equivalent to the shaded fraction?
Answer: 1/2
Working: 4 parts out of 8 is 4/8. 4 is half of 8, so 4/8 is equivalent to 1/2.

Question 4: A paper strip is folded into 10 equal parts and 6 parts are shaded. An identical paper strip is folded into 5 equal parts. How many parts must be shaded to show the equivalent fraction?
Answer: 3
Working: 6/10 is equivalent to 3/5 because 6 ÷ 2 = 3 and 10 ÷ 2 = 5. So 3 parts must be shaded.

--- Fraction equivalence using bar models ---

Question 5: Two bar models have the same total length. The first bar model is split into 4 equal parts with 2 parts shaded. The second bar model is split into 8 equal parts. How many parts must be shaded on the second bar model to equal 2/4?
Answer: 4
Working: 2/4 is equivalent to 4/8, so 4 parts must be shaded.

Question 6: Bar Model A is split into 3 equal blocks with 2 blocks shaded to represent 2/3. Bar Model B is the same length but split into 6 equal blocks. How many blocks must be shaded in Bar Model B to show an equivalent fraction?
Answer: 4
Working: Each third is equal to two sixths. 2 thirds = 4 sixths (2/3 = 4/6), so 4 blocks must be shaded.

Question 7: On a fraction wall, a bar of 1 whole is divided into 5 equal parts, and 2 parts are shaded (2/5). An identical bar below it is divided into tenths. How many tenths are equal in length to 2/5?
Answer: 4
Working: 1/5 is equal to 2/10, so 2/5 is equal to 4/10. There are 4 tenths.

Question 8: A bar model of length 1 whole is split into 8 equal parts, and 6 parts are shaded. Another bar model of the same length is split into 4 equal parts. What fraction of the second bar model has the same shaded length?
Answer: 3/4
Working: 6/8 is equivalent to 3/4 because dividing both numerator and denominator by 2 gives 3/4.

--- Fraction equivalence using simplest forms ---

Question 9: Write the fraction 2/4 in its simplest form.
Answer: 1/2
Working: Divide the numerator (2) and denominator (4) by 2: 2 ÷ 2 = 1 and 4 ÷ 2 = 2, giving 1/2.

Question 10: Write the fraction 2/6 in its simplest form.
Answer: 1/3
Working: Divide both numerator and denominator by 2: 2 ÷ 2 = 1 and 6 ÷ 2 = 3, giving 1/3.

Question 11: Write the fraction 4/8 in its simplest form.
Answer: 1/2
Working: Divide both numerator and denominator by 4: 4 ÷ 4 = 1 and 8 ÷ 4 = 2, giving 1/2.

Question 12: Write the fraction 6/8 in its simplest form.
Answer: 3/4
Working: Divide both numerator and denominator by 2: 6 ÷ 2 = 3 and 8 ÷ 2 = 4, giving 3/4.

Question 13: Write the fraction 4/10 in its simplest form.
Answer: 2/5
Working: Divide both numerator and denominator by 2: 4 ÷ 2 = 2 and 10 ÷ 2 = 5, giving 2/5.
