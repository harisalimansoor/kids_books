# STRICT QUESTION-BASED CURVED IMAGE GENERATION PROMPT

## 1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Generate one high-quality, natural-looking, photorealistic scene for a children's educational workbook. The scene must be based on the complete question provided at runtime and must use the user-uploaded reference photographs for all specified people.

The generated image must contain ONLY the illustration/photo asset required for the question. Do not generate an entire workbook page, worksheet, poster, or page design.

The final artwork must use a large, asymmetrical, organically curved silhouette, similar to a flowing hand-crafted cutout. It must be designed specifically for placement on the right side and lower portion of an A4 portrait workbook page, leaving the left side available for separately rendered question text and answer options.

**The question determines the scene. The uploaded photographs determine the people's identities. The organic curved silhouette determines the artwork's outer boundary. All three requirements are mandatory.**

## 2. READ AND UNDERSTAND THE COMPLETE QUESTION FIRST

Read the entire question from the first word to the last word before planning or generating the image. Never generate an image based only on the first few words, a single keyword, or a superficial interpretation.

Identify the following from the complete question:

- Every person or character mentioned or clearly implied, including their relationship, role and identity.
- The actual activity taking place, including what each person is doing and how they are interacting.
- The objects, tools, materials, food, toys, equipment or other props required by the activity.
- The most appropriate environment and background for the complete situation.
- The physical actions, body positions, hand movements, gaze direction and facial expressions that make the scene believable.
- The relevant educational context, such as counting, building, cooking, playing, gardening, sharing, drawing or performing another task.

Convert the question's complete meaning into one coherent visual moment.

For example, if the question describes a grandfather and a child using wooden blocks to construct something, show both people naturally engaged in building with blocks. If the question describes two people preparing food, place them in a suitable kitchen and show them preparing food. If they are playing football, depict actual football play in an appropriate outdoor location. If the activity involves planting a tree, show the people doing gardening work in a believable outdoor environment.

These are examples of scene interpretation, not fixed scenes. Always follow the actual question supplied at runtime.

Do not invent an unrelated activity, setting, object or story. Do not simply place the required people beside one another without showing the activity described by the question.

If the question contains several connected actions, choose one clear, visually meaningful moment that best communicates the overall situation. Keep the image suitable for young children and educational use.

## 3. USE THE USER-UPLOADED PEOPLE REFERENCES CORRECTLY

Use the supplied reference photographs to reproduce the actual people who are intended to appear in the scene.

Preserve each person's recognizable identity, including:

- Natural facial structure and face shape.
- Eyes, eyebrows, nose, lips and other distinctive facial characteristics.
- Natural skin tone and complexion.
- Hair colour, hairline, texture and recognizable hairstyle.
- Approximate age, age-related features and overall physical appearance.
- Distinctive identity characteristics visible in the supplied photograph.

The generated person must look recognizably like the corresponding person in the user's reference photograph. Do not create a generic replacement person or substantially redesign their face.

If the question describes a grandfather and grandchild, sibling pair, brother and sister, parent and child, or another relationship, use the supplied relationship information and corresponding photographs to assign the correct identities. Do not guess which photograph represents which person when explicit mapping is available.

Include only the people required by the question and the supplied character information. Never introduce additional people merely to make the scene look busier. Never duplicate, clone, merge or accidentally repeat a person.

When multiple people are supplied, preserve each individual's distinct identity. Do not transfer one person's face, hair, skin tone, age characteristics or other identifying features to another person.

The people must look naturally integrated into the same environment, with consistent camera perspective, realistic scale, matching lighting and believable physical interaction.

## 4. REMOVE UNRELATED OBJECTS FROM THE CHILD'S REFERENCE PHOTOGRAPH

Inspect the child's reference photograph before constructing the scene.

If the child is holding a mobile phone, toy, bottle, object, accessory or any other item in the original photograph, do not automatically reproduce that item in the generated scene. Remove the unrelated held object from the visual interpretation of the reference photograph.

Reconstruct the child's hands, fingers, wrists and arm positions naturally where necessary. Do not leave unnatural gripping gestures, partially erased objects, missing fingers, distorted palms or hands that still appear to be holding something invisible.

Only introduce objects that are genuinely needed for the activity described in the complete question. For example, a child may appropriately hold a wooden block while building, a spoon while cooking or a football while playing. These must be new, context-appropriate activity props, not an unnecessary copy of an unrelated object from the original photograph.

Treat every question as a fresh scene. Do not carry an object, toy, accessory or activity prop from a previous workbook image into a new image unless the new question independently requires that object.

Clothing may be changed or its colour may be adjusted to suit the activity and environment. However, clothing changes must never compromise the person's recognizable identity, natural age, body proportions or skin tone.

## 5. NATURAL HUMAN APPEARANCE AND PHOTOREALISM

The finished scene must look like a believable, high-quality photograph of real people engaged in a real activity.

Use natural human anatomy, realistic body proportions, authentic skin texture, natural hair, believable fabric, physically plausible object contact and lifelike environmental details.

Skin tones must remain natural and consistent with the supplied photographs. Do not whiten, excessively lighten, darken, artificially brighten or heavily airbrush the person's complexion. Do not apply excessive skin smoothing, plastic-looking skin, beauty filters or an artificial glossy finish. Preserve subtle natural skin texture and realistic facial details.

Avoid cartoon characters, anime, exaggerated children's-book faces, oversized eyes, doll-like expressions, painted skin, plastic-looking features and obviously computer-generated people. Do not transform the reference photographs into caricatures.

Facial expressions must suit the actual activity. People should appear naturally interested, attentive, engaged, curious, happy, focused or appropriately serious according to the situation. Expressions must look authentic rather than exaggerated or artificially cheerful.

Children must look like children of their actual age. Adults and older people must retain believable adult or age-related characteristics. Do not make an older person look unusually young or make a child look like an adult.

Maintain correct anatomy, natural posture, plausible balance, proper joint positioning and realistic interaction with nearby objects. Arms, shoulders, elbows, wrists, fingers, legs and feet must connect correctly and remain proportionate.

Hands must interact with objects properly. A person lifting a block must grip it naturally; a person preparing food must handle kitchen utensils correctly; a person playing a sport must use a believable action pose.

Every person must look comfortable and physically stable. Avoid twisted torsos, broken-looking wrists, reversed joints, unnaturally bent limbs, floating hands, awkward necks, impossible sitting positions and exaggerated body bends.

## 6. AUTHENTIC ACTIVITY, BODY LANGUAGE AND CAMERA DIRECTION

People must appear genuinely involved in the activity rather than posing for a photograph.

Show each person performing the correct action in response to the complete question. Use natural interaction, realistic contact with the activity objects and appropriate body positioning.

Direct the eyes and faces toward the activity, another person or a relevant object whenever appropriate. Do not automatically make every person look directly into the camera.

Camera-facing expressions or eye contact are permitted only when they naturally suit the activity. Otherwise, capture the scene as if a photographer were documenting a real moment without interrupting the people.

Avoid stiff standing poses, artificial smiles, identical facial expressions, front-facing portrait arrangements and unnatural hand placements. Do not make the people look bored, disconnected, frightened, crying or distracted unless the question genuinely requires that emotional context.

Keep the activity immediately understandable. The viewer should be able to see what the people are doing without needing to guess.

## 7. ENVIRONMENT MUST MATCH THE COMPLETE QUESTION

Construct the background only after interpreting the complete activity.

Choose an appropriate environment and include enough context to make the action believable. Cooking belongs in a suitable kitchen; outdoor games belong in an appropriate outdoor play area; gardening belongs in a garden or similar setting; indoor construction or table activities require an appropriate indoor environment.

The background, lighting, furniture, surfaces, tools and supporting objects must all make sense together.

Use realistic environmental depth and perspective. Keep the background visually coherent without allowing decorative objects to distract from the people or the activity.

Avoid mixing incompatible settings, such as kitchen cabinets in a garden, indoor furniture floating outdoors, unrelated sporting equipment in a cooking scene or arbitrary bookshelves in an environment where they do not belong.

Do not invent excessive background decoration, unnecessary toys, random food items, unrelated animals or extra props merely to fill empty space.

The environment should be detailed enough to feel real but visually controlled enough for a children's maths workbook.

## 8. STRICT ASYMMETRICAL ORGANIC CURVED SILHOUETTE

This is one of the most important requirements.

The artwork MUST have a large, irregular, organically flowing curved outer silhouette inspired by the supplied workbook reference. The shape must feel like a naturally designed, asymmetrical cutout rather than a rectangle, square, circle, oval, standard arch or regular blob.

The scene must be composed for placement on the right side of an A4 portrait page:

- Begin the visible artwork near the upper-right portion of the intended page layout.
- Allow the scene to extend generously along the right edge and downward toward the bottom of the page.
- Shape the inner left boundary with a smooth, asymmetrical curve that gradually moves inward and outward at different heights.
- Let the curve sweep toward the center-left in the lower portion of the layout where appropriate, then flow naturally across the bottom toward the right.
- Maintain a large, continuous visual area for the actual scene rather than creating a narrow strip or a tiny decorative image.
- Keep the upper-left and left-middle page area available for separately rendered question text and answer options.
- The organic silhouette may extend farther toward the left near the bottom, following the reference layout, but must not intrude into the intended text and answer-option area.
- Use smooth, flowing, irregular curves with natural variation. The left boundary must not look symmetrical or mechanically repeated.

The artwork must feel as though it naturally occupies the right and lower sections of a workbook page. It must not look like a rectangular photograph that has simply been placed inside a decorative frame.

Do not draw an outline around the organic shape. Do not use a visible stroke, border, frame, artificial shadow around the entire cutout, sticker outline or thick white contour.

Keep the scene's visual content inside the organic silhouette. Faces, hair, hands, arms, bodies and important activity objects must remain safely inside its curved boundary, with enough breathing room to prevent accidental cropping.

If transparency is supported, keep the area outside the curved silhouette transparent. If transparency is not supported, use a clean, pure-white background outside the curved shape so the artwork can blend into a white workbook page without a visible rectangular edge.

The white or transparent area outside the artwork must not become part of the depicted environment. It is simply the space outside the cutout.

## 9. LARGE, WELL-DISTRIBUTED COMPOSITION

The artwork must be visually substantial, not tiny, miniature, postage-stamp-sized or confined to the upper-right corner.

Use the intended A4 portrait placement as the composition guide: 210 × 297 mm. This describes the target workbook layout, not permission to generate a complete worksheet page.

Keep the main people large enough for a young child to recognize them easily. Their expressions, activity and interactions must remain readable at normal printed workbook size.

Distribute the visual elements naturally. Keep the primary people and the most important action in the center-right area. Place supporting objects where they would realistically be used, especially across the lower portion of the scene when suitable.

Use a medium-wide or wider environmental composition as required by the activity. Show enough of the people and their surroundings to explain what is happening. Do not default to a close-up portrait or crop the activity out of view.

Avoid excessive empty space inside the visible artwork, especially in its lower portion. Do not leave a large blank internal band, create a disproportionately small scene or concentrate all meaningful content in the upper half.

The image should extend through the available curved region in a balanced way, with a clear visual connection between the upper and lower portions. All important subjects must fit comfortably inside the shape rather than being pushed against its boundary.

## 10. LIGHTING, COLOUR AND PRINT QUALITY

Use natural, coherent lighting appropriate to the environment. Indoor scenes should have believable indoor illumination; outdoor scenes should have natural daylight or another contextually appropriate light source.

Maintain realistic skin colour, balanced contrast, natural shadows and believable colour relationships between people, objects and surroundings.

Use clear, attractive colours suitable for a children's educational workbook without making the scene overly saturated, neon-coloured, glossy or artificial.

Ensure that the main activity remains visually prominent against the background. Avoid excessive blur, harsh artificial highlights, extreme depth-of-field effects, oversharpening and cinematic darkness that obscures important details.

Produce clean, high-resolution artwork suitable for placement in a professionally printed workbook. Preserve fine details in faces, hands, hair, clothing and the objects used in the activity.

The scene must remain clear when scaled to its intended print size.

## 11. OUTPUT SCOPE: IMAGE ASSET ONLY

Generate only the required curved scene asset.

Do NOT generate a complete workbook page, even if the target placement is based on an A4 page. Do not draw a white worksheet panel with text on it. Do not compose the question or its options.

Do not add:

- Question text, instructions, words, letters or captions.
- Numbers, equations, calculations or mathematical answers.
- Multiple-choice options, checkboxes, answer circles or tick marks.
- Page numbers, grade labels, headings, titles or badges.
- Company names, logos, watermarks, website addresses or advertisements.
- Borders around the page, page corners, paper textures or a rectangular photo frame.
- Stickers, decorative labels, unrelated icons or unnecessary accessories.

The workbook's text, page number and question layout are predefined and will be added separately by the application.

The A4 portrait layout is only a positioning reference. Never reproduce the complete page as part of the generated asset.

Return one cohesive scene with one continuous asymmetrical organic curved silhouette, using the correct people, the correct action and the correct environment.

## 12. FINAL QUALITY CHECK BEFORE GENERATION

Before finalizing the image, verify internally that:

1. The entire question has been interpreted, not merely its opening words.
2. Every depicted person corresponds to the correct user-provided reference.
3. No required person is missing, duplicated or replaced with an invented person.
4. The activity clearly matches the question.
5. The background matches the activity and contains no contradictory environmental details.
6. Any unrelated object originally held by the child has been removed from the reference-photo interpretation.
7. Any new object in the scene is appropriate for the activity.
8. Faces, skin tones, hair, body proportions and recognizable identities remain natural.
9. Postures, fingers, limbs, object contact and facial expressions are physically believable.
10. People look engaged in their activity instead of automatically posing for the camera.
11. The scene is large, detailed and appropriately distributed throughout the curved region.
12. The outer silhouette is genuinely asymmetric and organically curved, not rectangular or geometrically regular.
13. No important face, hand, body part or activity object is clipped by the curved boundary.
14. The asset contains no question text, answers, page numbers, logos or other workbook layout elements.
15. The composition is suitable for use in an A4 portrait workbook measuring 210 × 297 mm.

If any of these requirements are violated, correct the composition before producing the final result.
