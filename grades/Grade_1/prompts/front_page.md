## 3. PAGE SIZE, ORIENTATION AND PRINTING

Create a portrait-oriented A4 front cover.

- Physical page size: 210 × 297 mm.
- Aspect ratio: approximately 0.707:1.
- Keep all essential content inside the page boundaries.
- Maintain a consistent safe-printing margin.
- Do not generate a landscape cover, square image, oversized canvas or an image that exceeds the specified page proportions.
- Do not crop the company name, title, child's name or website.
- Do not introduce a separate outer poster, mockup, book-in-perspective presentation or surrounding desktop scene.
- The output must represent the actual front-cover artwork, not a photograph of a physical book.

Keep the main illustration large enough to dominate the cover. Avoid excessive blank areas, tiny central artwork, unnecessary empty bands and oversized decorative borders.

The composition must feel complete from top to bottom, with the child's portrait, educational environment and supporting elements integrated into the page.

## 5. CHILD PHOTOGRAPH: IDENTITY AND QUALITY PRESERVATION

The uploaded photograph is the authoritative identity reference for the child.

Reconstruct or integrate the child into a natural, high-quality photographic scene while preserving their recognizable identity.

Preserve as accurately as possible:
- Facial identity and overall facial structure.
- Skin tone and natural skin texture.
- Eye shape, eye colour where discernible, and natural eye placement.
- Nose, lips, cheeks, chin and other recognizable facial features.
- Hair colour, texture and general hairstyle.
- Apparent age and childlike proportions.
- Distinctive identity features visible in the reference photograph.

Do not replace the child with a generic AI-generated child. Do not substantially change their facial structure, ethnicity, apparent age or recognizable appearance.

The result should look like a high-quality, professionally photographed version of the same child in a suitable educational environment.

### Expression correction

If the original photograph shows an expression unsuitable for a cheerful workbook cover, make a restrained, natural correction.

Examples:
- If the child looks sad, crying or upset, create a calm, comfortable expression.
- If the child appears bored or disengaged, create a naturally attentive and interested expression.
- If the child looks angry, distressed or visibly uncomfortable, soften the expression into a relaxed, friendly appearance.
- If the original expression is already pleasant and natural, preserve it rather than unnecessarily modifying it.

Maintain the child's identity while making only the changes needed to improve the expression.

The preferred expression is:
- Relaxed and emotionally comfortable.
- Naturally cheerful or pleasantly focused.
- Engaged in the activity.
- Appropriate for the child's age.
- Realistic rather than exaggerated.

Do not create an artificial, excessively wide smile, unnaturally enlarged eyes, exaggerated facial features, plastic-looking skin or a forced advertising expression.

The child must look like a real child participating comfortably in a real activity.

## 6. REMOVE OBJECTS FROM THE ORIGINAL PHOTOGRAPH

Do not blindly reproduce the entire original photograph.

If the child is holding a phone, toy, unrelated object, food item, bag, book or any other object in the uploaded photograph, do not automatically preserve it.

Instead:
1. Extract and preserve the child's recognizable identity.
2. Remove the original held object when it is unrelated to the newly selected activity.
3. Reconstruct the hands, arms and clothing naturally where required.
4. Create a new, physically plausible pose suitable for the workbook topic.
5. Introduce only the educational materials needed for the new scene.

For example, if the original child is holding a mobile phone and the selected topic is mathematics, the final scene may show the same child naturally arranging counting blocks or writing in a workbook.

Do not leave ghost objects, partially removed objects, distorted fingers, fused hands, broken wrists, duplicate fingers or unnatural gaps where the original object was removed.

The new pose must be anatomically plausible and consistent with the child's body position.

The objective is to preserve the child, not to preserve unrelated objects from the source photograph.

## 7. NATURAL BODY LANGUAGE AND ACTIVE PARTICIPATION

The child must be the main visual subject and must appear actively involved in the selected educational activity.

Avoid a passive, bored, rigid or mannequin-like pose.

Suitable actions include:
- Writing or drawing naturally on a workbook.
- Counting educational objects.
- Arranging number blocks.
- Exploring shapes or patterns.
- Measuring objects.
- Comparing quantities.
- Reading or interacting with appropriate learning materials.
- Participating in another activity directly related to the selected topics.

Select an action that makes physical and contextual sense.

Hands must interact naturally with the objects. The child's gaze should follow the task, the learning material, the objects or another participant when appropriate.

Do not force the child to stare directly into the camera. A natural candid, three-quarter or activity-focused pose is preferred.

The body posture should communicate curiosity, confidence, comfort and active engagement.

Maintain realistic proportions, natural shoulders, believable arm positions, correctly formed hands and physically plausible interaction with the environment.

## 8. BACKGROUND AND ENVIRONMENT MUST MATCH THE SELECTED TOPICS

Build the background from the user's selected educational topics and workbook category.

Do not use one fixed classroom background for every cover. Select an environment that naturally supports the activities.

Examples:
- Counting, addition or subtraction: a bright learning table with counting blocks, counters or a suitable workbook.
- Shapes and patterns: geometric toys, colourful shape blocks and simple construction materials.
- Measurement: rulers, measuring objects and a suitable activity surface.
- Time: an appropriate learning clock and related materials.
- Money and coins: realistic educational play money or suitable counting materials.
- Science: a child-safe exploration table with appropriate scientific objects.
- Reading and language: books, picture cards and a comfortable reading area.
- Art: a creative workspace with paper and suitable art materials.
- Sports-related learning: an appropriate outdoor or indoor activity space where the child is visibly participating.
- Mixed educational topics: a coherent learning environment that combines a small number of relevant elements without becoming cluttered.

These are examples, not mandatory props. Only include objects that are relevant to the actual selected topics.

The environment must feel physically connected to the child. Objects should have correct scale, perspective, lighting and contact shadows.

Use a believable real-world setting, such as a bright learning corner, an attractive classroom, a home study area or another topic-appropriate environment.

The background should support the child rather than compete with them.
